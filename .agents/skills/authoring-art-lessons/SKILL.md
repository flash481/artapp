# Author art lessons

**Default worker: Luna Max** (`gpt-6-luna` at max reasoning when selectable). Sol owns or approves the objective, prerequisites, and course-level intent; Luna turns that brief into a clear, usable lesson. This follows the project's objective to lower aggregate token/compute cost by routing high-volume drafting to capable Luna workers and reserving Sol for high-leverage course decisions; it is not a measured price claim. The identifier is a task/agent choice, not repository routing configuration.

Do not invent or silently revise the curriculum sequence. If the objective, prerequisite, or source basis is unclear, flag the gap for Sol. For approved work, follow `docs/LESSON_SCHEMA.md` and include its requested metadata and provenance.

Structure learner-facing content as: objective → relevant 5–10 minute warm-up → concise concept explanation → useful visual material → substantial main drawing exercise → a few targeted self-review questions → optional extension. Normal lessons should take about 30–60 minutes and usually produce a recognizable drawing. Keep explanation only as long as needed to do the exercise. Make materials, steps, and success criteria concrete; connect the warm-up to the objective and the review questions to the targeted fundamentals.

Preserve the lesson's intended subject and scaffolding. Draw on observational practice early; use stylized subjects as occasional applications rather than substitutes for foundations. Attribute adapted exercises and visuals, distinguish source-derived ideas from original lesson directions, and do not reproduce long passages or whole illustrations. Draft in batches with consistent fields so Luna can handle routine production and Sol can review representative or difficult lessons.
