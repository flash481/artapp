# Personal drawing education — Phase 2B architecture

The course has **72 self-paced lessons** in six navigation sections of 12. These are sequence markers, not weeks or deadlines. Most lessons plan for 45 minutes; seven integration projects are longer (six at 90 minutes and the final at 150 minutes). The learner draws in a sketchbook while a short phone lesson directs attention. The [complete lesson map](../curriculum/lesson-map.json) is the source of truth for Phase 2C objectives, exercise intents, prerequisites, media, visual needs, source links, and learner decisions. It is planning data, not finished lesson prose or app content.

| Lessons | Section | Main transition |
|---|---|---|
| 01–12 | Look and draw | A mug, shoe, plant, flower, fruit, cup, and window view make observation, line, measurement, value, form, first composition, and depth concrete. The first stylised exercise is a small transfer task. |
| 13–24 | Build form and light | Comparison, volumes, petals, eye level, hatching, edges, and two-point space return in real objects. The first project is an observed still life. |
| 25–36 | Make space and design | Trees, atmosphere, crop, rotated characters, thumbnails, charcoal value masses, plants, interiors, texture, and landscape combine. A sense-of-place project closes the section. |
| 37–48 | Draw people and movement | Gesture precedes mass and selective anatomy; heads are adjustable constructions checked against individual observation. Object design and landscape studies interrupt the people sequence. Portrait light leads to a figure or portrait project. |
| 49–60 | Combine and interpret | Two analytical master studies transfer line or value decisions into original subjects. Hands with fabric, figure in room, charcoal gesture, natural forms, character action, and landscape marks lead to natural-form and landscape projects. |
| 61–72 | Choose and communicate | Subject, viewpoint, composition, medium, focal point, and interpretation become learner decisions. The learner plans and makes an independent final drawing. |

## How the spiral works

The course introduces fundamentals quickly, then revisits them through a different question or subject. A three-value fruit in lesson 07 becomes light on a rounded object (14), a still-life decision (24), atmospheric distance (26 and 35), portrait light (45 and 58), and independent value planning (65, 71–72). Early crop choice (06) becomes still-life hierarchy (23–24), landscape thumbnails (29), person/place design (48 and 60), and independent projects (68–72). Observation and construction remain paired: the learner looks, builds a simple model when useful, then checks that model against the subject.

The [concept graph](../curriculum/concept-graph.json) uses the 25 Phase 2A canonical concept IDs and rolls them into 12 auditable strands. The [prerequisite graph](../curriculum/prerequisites.json) records enabling experiences rather than mastery gates. The [coverage matrix](../curriculum/coverage.json) names every lesson behind each `introduced`, `practised`, `revisited`, `combined`, or `independent` stage. Stage counts describe intended course opportunities, not proof of learner mastery. Specialist concepts such as drapery receive proportionate coverage; central strands return frequently.

## Exercise, subject, and material progression

Drawing from life is the default: mugs, shoes, plants, flowers, books, interiors, hands, personal objects, and nearby views. Supplied references are planned where lighting, a timed figure pose, or a specific landscape problem makes them useful. Source exercises are paraphrased and adapted to one sketchbook session, with their original IDs in the map. [Exercise](../curriculum/exercise-audit.json), [subject](../curriculum/subject-audit.json), and [scaffolding](../curriculum/scaffolding-audit.json) audits make the distribution explicit.

Three required flower applications are spaced through the first half of the course: measurement (04), overlapping form (16), and light on petals (34). A late vessel arrangement permits flowers as a learner choice (66). Other natural-form work uses plants, leaves, shells, and ordinary still-life objects, reflecting the source corpus's general methods rather than claiming a dedicated botanical method. Landscapes begin with overlap and scale, then grow through trees, atmosphere, thumbnails, buildings, selective marks, and personally meaningful places. Four stylised character applications progress from large shape comparison to rotation, action, and original creature design. Portrait work starts after basic observation, proportion, and form, while figure anatomy stays limited to gesture, weight, major masses, landmarks, and useful joints.

Graphite carries the early course. Charcoal first appears in lesson 30 to make broad value masses and recurs for landscape atmosphere, portrait light, gesture, edge control, and expressive portraiture. From the first integration projects onward, medium choice gradually becomes the learner's decision. Colour painting is outside this course.

## Projects and finish

The seven project lessons are 24 (observed still life), 36 (sense of place), 48 (figure or portrait), 59 (natural forms), 60 (landscape interpretation), 68 (personal place), and 72 (final project). The final project begins with a separate planning lesson (71). The learner chooses a meaningful subject, gathers lawful or self-made references, tests thumbnails and values, chooses graphite or charcoal and a focal point, completes the drawing, then evaluates what to practise next.

Source material receives a recorded placement or omission in [source-mapping.json](../curriculum/source-mapping.json). The [decision record](../curriculum/CURRICULUM_DECISIONS.md) explains synthesis choices and source limits. Book files, analyses, extracts, and research remain private under `sources/`; no source illustration is approved for deployment by its appearance in this map.
