# Core Drawing architecture — final red-team review

**Status:** This report reviews the proposed 130-lesson map and supporting source and visual plans. The sequence is planning-only and awaits human review. Pilot Lessons 01–09 remain unchanged prototype/reference material; no lesson prose, JSON, app code, or image asset was produced here.

## Review method

Three independent reviewer roles—beginner/graphite readiness, form/space transfer, and figure/anatomy/visual coverage—read the former plan and then the rebuilt map. A final read checked that actionable corrections appeared in the current files. This is architectural rather than empirical; learner response during an approved pilot will still matter.

## Findings and decisions

| Review area | Finding | Resolution in the current plan |
|---|---|---|
| Fresh start and graphite readiness | Pilot numbering/content could still constrain Lesson 01; paper care, pressure, point/side, erasure and first tone needed clearer order. | The map starts with a new baseline and teaches light marks, pressure, pencil contact, correction and paper care in 01–04. Pilot assets remain unchanged and noncanonical. Tone begins at 29–31 before value thumbnails at 32. |
| Early beginner access | Photo fallbacks began before photo limitations were taught; the baseline view might not be reproducible; unit sighting and first stable-light setup were under-specified. | Lesson 01 introduces fixed-view/crop/light checks before photo-based work and asks the learner to save the drawing and keep or recreate its view. Lesson 14 sights and transfers a ratio at a steady viewpoint. Lesson 29 uses one steady side light. Lesson 121 develops photo literacy further. |
| Form and construction | Form needed applied solids, joins, limits of simplified models, same-view correction and transfer into rooms, people and irregular subjects. | Lessons 34–47 build and test masses/axes; 48–60 teach observed space and perspective; later figure, hand and natural-form work applies construction. Lesson 42 asks where a model stops helping. |
| Perspective and foreshortening | A perspective diagram must not override observed space; a late room task must succeed without it; foreshortening needed object-to-limb transfer. | Lessons 48–57 build depth from observed placement, overlap, scale and contact. Lessons 58–59 apply cylinder/object foreshortening, 81–82 limbs, 103 the hand and 127 a full figure. Lesson 125 draws a room without relying on a diagram. |
| Figure structure | Prior coverage lacked clear joints/landmarks/connections, foot construction and a required full-figure study. | Lessons 73–83 teach balance, proportion, torso masses, joints and connected limbs; 83 requires a full-body/three-quarter study. Lessons 104–108 teach hand/foot mass and support; 127 requires figure-in-space application. |
| Head, likeness and drapery | Generic head construction alone did not ensure sitter-specific likeness; clothing should not require head studies unless the task uses them. | Lessons 92–99 progress from mass to observed features and same-sitter correction; 99 is a sustained portrait. Lessons 116–118 teach fold causes and clothing; 118 no longer lists head/likeness as a prerequisite. Lesson 128 integrates likeness and clothing in a bounded crop. |
| Composition, simplification and self-critique | Composition was overclaimed in every exercise; some correction tasks changed subject and failed to test a correction; late review needed a focal/frame check. | The map names composition teaching/returns instead of claiming universality. Lessons 91 and 120 include frame/focal review; 119–130 use baseline comparison, same-subject correction, transfer and independent choice. Lesson 123 specifies four three-minute thumbnails and one developed choice. |
| Optional tools and physical technique | Multiple erasers or extra graphite grades could be mistaken as required. | One-eraser routes are explicit at 04 and 70; HB completes every lesson. Optional tools and grades remain optional. |
| Source locators | The 130 map and source crosswalk needed exact alignment for Hampton’s arm, leg, head, hand and foot material. | The map and companion cite H09 arm pp. 129–159, leg pp. 178–201, head pp. 56–85, hand pp. 160–177 and foot pp. 202–217; LH11 hand pp. 129–149 and head pp. 22–35, 44–69. Module 6 includes N99 pp. 1–154. |
| Visual coverage | New lesson IDs had no matching manifest while the prior manifest named the superseded 93-lesson sequence. | The manifest now specifies original visual direction, prompt and alt-text intent for each of the 130 IDs. It is planning only; no image is generated or selected as final here. |
| Human-subject scope | Figure, portrait, hand and foot work occupies a substantial portion of this general course. | Retained because the request specifically calls for usable figure structure, likeness, hands/feet and transfer. The scope is explicit for human review: 34 lessons and about 27 h 15 min, roughly 27% of planned practice time. |

## Duration and workload audit

The map contains 130 lesson entries and 6,105 scheduled minutes: 9 Focused Studies at 30 minutes, 111 Standard Lessons at 45 minutes, and 10 Extended Studies at 75–90 minutes. The extended lessons are 13, 33, 47, 60, 72, 83, 99, 115, 127 and 130; all now have explicit extended-study titles. The total is approximately **101 hours 45 minutes**, including setup, drawing, comparison and review.

People-focused work at 19–20, 46, 73–83, 92–108, 118 and 127–128 totals about **1,635 minutes (27 h 15 min)**. This is defensible against the requested depth but remains a human scope decision, not an empirical optimum.

## Remaining review questions
