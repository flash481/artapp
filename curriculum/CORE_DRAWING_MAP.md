# ArtApp Core Drawing — 93-lesson curriculum architecture and production map

**Canonical current architecture; planning only.** This is the single current lesson sequence and production map. It is not lesson copy and does not approve lesson production. The enriched pilot material is reconciled by retention, movement and merger; its finished JSON and existing visual assets are not edited in this task. Do not generate planned assets yet. Superseded 72-lesson materials are archived under `curriculum/archive/phase-2b/` and are historical only.

**Field convention:** every lesson lists module/cluster, primary and reinforced concepts, true prerequisites, type/duration, subject, life/reference mode, fallback, exercise, optional concept-specific practice, deliberate revisits, Read More purpose, source/further-reading locators, placement reason, and visual IDs. Expanded visual prompts, source-image candidates, and fallback photo briefs are in `CORE_DRAWING_VISUAL_MANIFEST.md`. The complete bibliographic key follows the map.

**Types and time:** Focused Study = 25 min; Standard Lesson = 45 min; Extended Study = the row-specific 75–120 min. Targets include cue/setup, drawing, comparison and review; they are not all drawing time. In a Focused Study, keep cue/setup brief (about 4–5 min total), include any warm-up in the exercise, and reserve time to compare/correct. Optional reinforcement adds 10–20 min and is never a progression gate. Extended studies introduce little or no major new theory, consolidate the named recent lessons and include a pause to compare and correct.
**Mode vocabulary:** Life = Life recommended; Ref = Reference recommended; Either = Either equally suitable; Both = Both intentionally used. Every Life row names its subject, a low-friction setup and a supplied-photo fallback; the visual manifest gives the photo/setup brief. For human subjects, a live model is optional; supplied references and self/mirror alternatives remain available.

## Module 1 — Observation and graphite line (Lessons 01–03)

Learners begin with a real object, a simple tool orientation, and a short cycle of seeing, marking, comparing and correcting. The first three studies establish enough physical control for later shape work without becoming a pencil-technique boot camp.

## Module 2 — Shape, gaps and visible relationships (Lessons 04–09)

The emphasis moves from whole silhouette to negative shapes, angles, overlaps and a sustained application. Previously learned line and correction habits stay active. The plant exercise is one ordinary observational subject, followed by a chair and mixed objects.

## Module 3 — Comparative measurement (Lessons 10–17)

Learners concentrate on units, overall proportion, intervals, angles, foreshortened appearance and group relationships, then apply a small set of checks in one extended study. The first measurement lesson combines useful pilot material with a learner-chosen personal object; a flower or stem remains optional.

## Module 4 — Composition as active choice (Lessons 18–25)

Crop, scale, negative space, overlap, tangency, hierarchy and thumbnails form a focused cluster. Composition remains a horizontal strand in every later drawing. Two-value thumbnails are a brief early application; full tonal control is taught in Module 7.

## Module 5 — Form, construction and ellipses (Lessons 26–33)

Box planes, ellipse axes, cylinders, cross-contour and connected volumes prepare an observed construction study. The existing cup exercise is placed here and joined to the deeper build/compare/rebuild study. Construction proposes; the observed silhouette and proportions decide what to keep.

## Module 6 — Perspective and spatial depth (Lessons 34–42)

Eye level, convergence, room planes, furniture, repeated intervals and foreshortening extend spatial reasoning from near objects to interior views. Short technical diagrams support, but do not replace, drawn applications and photographic literacy.

## Module 7 — Light, value and graphite control (Lessons 43–51)

A focused value cluster teaches grade choice, pressure, point/side, layered tone, parallel and crosshatching, eraser lifting, controlled blending and light logic through real objects. The pilot fruit study joins the first full value lesson here. An extended still life consolidates the methods.

## Module 8 — Human subjects (Lessons 52–64)

Supplied poses support gesture, weight, simple proportion and support, head construction/observation, hands, and a modest integrated study. Self/mirror and optional willing-model routes remain available; no exercise requires another person. Detailed anatomy and specialist portrait methods stay deferred.

## Module 9 — Edges, texture and mark economy (Lessons 65–72)

Learners use already taught pressure, layering, hatching, blending and eraser lifting to choose line hierarchy, hard/soft/lost edges, texture and detail. These are marks used in drawings, not isolated samples.

## Module 10 — Organic forms and fabric (Lessons 73–80)

A limited group of irregular forms, natural-object arrangements and cloth studies transfers construction, spacing, value and graphite mark choice without becoming a botanical or landscape course.

## Module 11 — Integration and independence (Lessons 81–93)

Retrieval, photo-versus-life comparison, reference inspection, viewpoint transfer, simplification and learner-chosen studies reduce scaffolding. Extended studies consolidate recent learning and allow explicit correction without requiring multi-day finished projects.

### 01. Your first drawing — observe, compare and correct

**Cluster:** observation and graphite line, 01–03. **Primary / reinforced:** observation and self-correction / contour, shape, pressure and graphite handling. **Prerequisites:** none. **Type/time:** existing Standard Lesson, 45 min. **Subject / mode:** mug from life / Life. **Fallback:** keep the same mug photo from the learner's viewpoint. **Exercise:** Keep the existing two-pass mug baseline and name one visible relation to improve. Add a brief pencil orientation on scrap: comfortable writing grip for short marks, steady page, a sharpened point for small contours, the side of the lead for a broad pale mark, light pressure, and one gentle eraser lift. Show how to notice a rounded point, rotate the pencil to find a sharper edge, and sharpen when small marks become blunt; a pre-sharpened pencil is fine. A relaxed writing grip is the default; holding farther back or loosely overhand for broad marks is optional, never a grip rule. Use one HB pencil; do not require extra tools. **Optional:** Repeat with a different household object, correcting only the selected relation. **Revisit:** pressure and pencil position return in 02–03, 05–08, 43–51 and 65–72; observation/correction recurs through every extended study and the independent sequence 81–93. **Read More:** keep the existing perceptual-error/correction deep dive; add why light marks preserve options, how pencil angle changes a mark, and why grip is functional rather than fixed. **Sources / further reading:** D85 pp. 10–39; WG18 pp. 15–23; P16 pp. 8–9, 21–23. **Placement:** establishes the observation loop and leaves a baseline while giving the beginner enough physical control to begin. **Planned fallback visual:** cd-l01-mug-reference. **Planned graphite visual:** `cd-m01-pencil-orientation`. **Visuals:** existing `lesson-01-observation-cycle`, `lesson-01-warmup-check-one-relation`, `lesson-01-two-pass-mug-study`.

### 02. Control the line — pressure, search and selection

**Cluster:** observation and graphite line, 01–03. **Primary / reinforced:** line quality, pressure range and mark selection / observation, contour, comparison and hand control. **Prerequisite:** 01. **Type/time:** existing Standard, 45 min. **Subject / mode:** book or key from life / Life. **Fallback:** fixed-view photo of the selected object. **Exercise:** Keep the existing searching-to-selected line practice and pressure warm-up. Calibrate light, medium and firm marks on scrap, keeping pressure below the level that dents paper; use a sharpened point for selected edges, compare point and side marks, and apply the choices to the familiar object. If the point rounds over, rotate the pencil to find a sharper edge or sharpen it when needed. **Optional:** use varied pressure to draw a folded cloth or tool. **Revisit:** contour application in 03; pressure, grade and layered tone in 43–51; line hierarchy and mark economy in 65–72. **Read More:** teach line as an adjustable mark rather than a speed test; add paper tooth, pressure, point/side and line-weight nuance without equating confidence with absence of correction. **Sources:** D85 pp. 40–69; A11 Ch. 3, “Types and Uses of Lines”; P16 pp. 8–9, 21–23, 32–35. **Placement:** moves existing pilot line-control practice ahead of contour and any task that expects the learner to vary pencil pressure. **Planned fallback visual:** cd-l05-book-reference. **Visuals:** existing `lesson-05-search-and-select-lines`, `lesson-05-pressure-warmup`, `lesson-05-lines-exercise`.

### 03. Follow an edge — contour turns and correction

**Cluster:** observation and graphite line, 01–03. **Primary / reinforced:** contour direction, line continuity and correction / observation, line economy, pressure. **Prerequisite:** 01–02. **Type/time:** existing Standard, 45 min. **Subject / mode:** shoe from life, with a short transfer on a spoon or peeler / Life. **Fallback:** ArtApp-owned side-view shoe photo, same crop. **Exercise:** Use the existing shoe contour lesson. After the checked pass, transfer the same question to six direction changes on a spoon or peeler: draw one slow contour, compare the tip/bowl/handle angles, and correct two turns. Keep the transfer brief so both subjects serve one contour skill. **Optional:** do a five-turn contour of a folded cloth or tool, changing subject but keeping the same criterion. **Revisit:** shape and gaps 04–06, spatial overlaps 08, measurement 10–17 and line/edge cluster 65–72. **Read More:** explain contour as a chosen boundary, where it disappears into value or overlap, when looking away helps, and how a second subject tests whether the learner is seeing turns rather than memorizing a shoe outline. **Sources:** WG18 pp. 24–37; E12 PDF pp. 121–154; P16 pp. 31–41. **Placement:** retains the finished contour lesson and folds in the later contour-turn study so early practice is followed immediately by a subject transfer instead of repeating contour much later. **Planned fallback visual:** cd-l02-shoe-reference. **Visuals:** existing `lesson-02-contour-turns`, `lesson-02-warmup-edge-checks`, `lesson-02-two-contour-passes`. **Existing pilot visuals:** lesson-02-contour-turns, lesson-02-warmup-edge-checks and lesson-02-two-contour-passes (unchanged). **Planned visual:** cd-m03-contour-turns.

### 04. One clear silhouette — big shapes before parts

**Cluster:** whole silhouette before gap and angle studies, 04–06. **Primary / reinforced:** silhouette and internal shape grouping / contour, negative space. **Prerequisite:** 03. **Type/time:** Focused Study, 25 min. **Subject / mode:** compact parcel with tape or a simple toy / Life. **Fallback:** single-view neutral photo. **Exercise:** block the whole outside shape, mark two interior divisions, cover details, compare width/height/tilt, then correct the silhouette. **Optional:** use a packaged food item; preserve the whole-to-part sequence. **Revisit:** negative shape and measurement 05–06, 10–17; construction 26–33. **Read More:** clarify why a silhouette is a useful first test but cannot describe every interior feature; connect positive and negative shape without a rigid recipe. **Sources:** WG18 pp. 24–37, 50–71; A11 Ch. 3, “The Block In”; BWZ18 pp. 6–9 (scaffold only). **Placement:** puts whole-shape checking before a learner is asked to use gaps as evidence. **Visual:** `cd-m04-silhouette-first`.

### 05. Shapes around a plant — negative space

**Cluster:** whole silhouette before gap and angle studies, 04–06. **Primary / reinforced:** negative space and silhouette / contour, comparison and spacing. **Prerequisite:** 03. **Type/time:** existing Standard, 45 min. **Subject / mode:** potted plant from life / Life. **Fallback:** fixed-view plant photo. **Exercise:** Draw the plant gaps as clear shapes bounded by observed edges; compare each opening by width, angle and position, then correct one surrounding leaf or pot edge. Keep the drawing about the gap shape and do not shade the opening. **Optional:** find two gaps in a chair or tool arrangement and compare their widths before drawing. **Revisit:** chair-gap transfer in 06; focused crop/gap studies 19–21; pressure and line direction 02, 43–51. **Read More:** teach the positive/negative relation and shared boundary; explain that a negative shape is a way to check the subject, not a separate object to render. **Sources:** E12 PDF pp. 155–179; P16 pp. 26–29. **Placement:** keeps the existing plant-gap subject after line control has been introduced and keeps an early plant study focused on seeing shape rather than introducing unrelated tone technique. **Planned fallback visual:** cd-l03-plant-reference. **Visuals:** existing `lesson-03-shared-edge-gap-concept`, `lesson-03-warmup-two-gaps`, `lesson-03-gap-first-plant-study`. **Existing pilot visual set:** lesson-03-shared-edge-gap-concept, lesson-03-warmup-two-gaps and lesson-03-gap-first-plant-study (unchanged).

### 06. Gaps in a chair — negative shapes as checks

**Cluster:** whole silhouette before gap and angle studies, 04–06. **Primary / reinforced:** negative space / contour, angles, proportion. **Prerequisite:** 05. **Type/time:** Standard, 45 min. **Subject / mode:** dining chair / Life. **Fallback:** straight-on chair photo. **Exercise:** draw page placement and two openings first; use their angles/widths to place seat, legs and back; compare/correct one gap. **Optional:** test one chair opening against a different chair, no full redraw. **Revisit:** crop and spacing studies 18–21; later figure/room gaps 45–64 and integrated drawings 81–93. **Read More:** explain shared borders and visual grouping; caution that the gap is evidence, not an abstract shape replacing the chair. **Sources:** E12 PDF pp. 155–179; P16 pp. 26–29; GW14 pp. 14–36. **Placement:** transfers negative-space looking to a structured subject with multiple openings. **Visual:** `cd-m06-chair-gaps`.

### 07. Compare the slant — angles and alignments

**Cluster:** angles, alignment and overlaps, 07–08. **Primary / reinforced:** angle comparison and plumb/level alignment / observation, contour and negative space. **Prerequisites:** 03, 06. **Type/time:** Standard, 45 min. **Subject / mode:** scissors and ruler arranged at a slight angle / Life. **Fallback:** top-down photo with both objects fully visible. **Exercise:** mark three dominant axes; compare their tilt to the page and to one another; test two alignments with an unmarked pencil; redraw the largest error. **Optional:** check one slant and one vertical on an appliance. **Revisit:** measurement 10–17; room and perspective work 34–42; figure pose direction 52–58. **Read More:** distinguish measured direction from decorative diagonal; explain plumb/level only relative to the chosen view. **Sources:** WG18 pp. 50–71; E12 PDF pp. 180–211; P16 pp. 26–29. **Placement:** gives the learner a qualitative angle/alignment check before the focused comparative-measurement cluster. **Visual:** `cd-m07-angle-alignments`.

### 08. Where forms meet — overlaps and interrupted edges

**Cluster:** angles, alignment and overlaps, 07–08. **Primary / reinforced:** overlap order and interrupted edges / contour, negative shape, depth. **Prerequisites:** 03, 06. **Type/time:** Standard, 45 min. **Subject / mode:** glass jar behind a small carton / Life. **Fallback:** ArtApp-owned photo at the same view. **Exercise:** place the large shapes, identify which contour stops at each overlap, draw the visible relationships and one through-line lightly, then correct a tangency. **Optional:** change which object is in front and sketch the altered overlap. **Revisit:** composition 19–25; construction 29–33; still-life studies 51, 80. **Read More:** explain T-junctions and occlusion as observational cues; distinguish a visible contour from a provisional hidden edge; no construction system is required yet. **Sources:** D85 pp. 128–147; P16 pp. 57–63; A11 Ch. 5, “Understanding Form”. **Placement:** teaches depth through visible interruptions before formal construction or perspective. **Visual:** `cd-m08-overlap-order`.

### 09. Extended Study — arrange, compare and correct

**Cluster:** opening-study consolidation, 01–08. **Primary / reinforced:** observation, silhouette, contour, negative shape, angles, overlap, selected line and correction. **Prerequisites:** 01–08. **Type/time:** Extended Study, 75 min. **Subject / mode:** one primary household object with one overlapping secondary form; a third object is optional / Life. **Fallback:** use the supplied two-object photo if setup is inconvenient. **Exercise:** 5-minute setup/cue; make a 3-minute memory silhouette of one object; use 10 minutes for thumbnails; spend 45 minutes drawing one primary object with one overlapping secondary form, including a midpoint compare/correct pause; use 12 minutes to review the memory sketch, name one changed strategy and record a next question. Total: 75 minutes. No gesture preview is required. **Optional:** 10-minute second crop of the same arrangement; or substitute one supplied adult full-pose reference for the object group and draw its broad silhouette and spacing (no live model). **Revisits:** consolidates 01–08 and absorbs the former observation-checkpoint cue; applies line selection from 02 and prepares for measurement 10–17. **Read More:** explain sustained looking, retrieval as a brief memory cue rather than a test, and how to choose one correction; the optional human-reference route applies the same observation skill without an extra person. **Sources:** D85 pp. 10–39, 40–69; WG18 pp. 15–23, 50–71; P16 pp. 98–104. **Placement:** first extended study consolidates only the concepts just introduced; the one-off four-pose gesture preview has been removed. **Visual:** `cd-m09-extended-correction-study`.

### 10. Choose a measuring unit — relative size and alignment

**Cluster:** unit and overall proportion, 10–12. **Primary / reinforced:** comparative measurement, temporary units and one angle/alignment check / negative space, silhouette, observation. **Prerequisite:** none; opening shape and angle work are useful prior practice, not gates. **Type/time:** Standard Lesson, 45 min. **Subject / mode:** learner-selected personal object with a clear simple shape or axis; a spoon is one option, and a flower or leafy stem is optional / Life. **Fallback:** fixed-view ArtApp-owned photo of the selected subject; retain a single-stem photo only for the optional plant route. **Exercise:** Choose a visible width as a provisional unit; compare height, one angle and one alignment; make a measured block-in and correct it against the object's whole silhouette. If the learner chooses a flower or stem, apply the same checks without botanical detail. **Optional:** repeat the same comparison with a flower or leafy stem if preferred. **Revisit:** concentrated at 11–17; applied to composition, form, perspective, people and independent studies. **Read More:** teach comparative units and fixed-viewpoint checks; explain why measured lengths/angles are comparisons rather than absolute numbers or ideal ratios. **Sources:** WG18 pp. 50–71; D85 pp. 70–101; P16 pp. 26–29; A11 Ch. 3, “The Block In” and “Measuring”. **Placement:** starts the focused measurement cluster and folds the useful pilot sighting material into a varied-subject lesson; flowers are optional, not a course strand. **Visual:** `cd-m10-unit-measure`. **Existing pilot material retained but not made a requirement:** lesson-04-relative-measurement, lesson-04-flower-warmup, lesson-04-observed-flower-block-in and lesson-04-measurement-mistake (unchanged; later content work should optionalise the flower-centered examples).

### 11. Check the whole before the parts — height, width and placement

**Cluster:** unit and overall proportion, 10–12. **Primary / reinforced:** overall proportion / measurement, silhouette, composition. **Prerequisite:** 10. **Type/time:** Focused Study, 25 min. **Subject / mode:** potato or egg / Life. **Fallback:** one pear photo at eye height. **Exercise:** place top/bottom/left/right; compare overall height to width; only then add the stem and one interior change. **Optional:** compare an egg and pear using separate units. **Revisit:** extended 17; organic forms 73–76. **Read More:** explain envelope-first drawing and why local detail can hide a whole-shape error; clarify that natural variation defeats idealized geometric templates. **Sources:** WG18 pp. 50–71; D85 pp. 70–101; A11 Ch. 3, “Measuring”. **Placement:** applies the unit method to an irregular natural object. **Visual:** `cd-m11-whole-proportion`.

### 12. Repeated intervals — compare spacing

**Cluster:** unit and overall proportion, 10–12. **Primary / reinforced:** interval and spacing / negative shape, alignment. **Prerequisite:** 06. **Type/time:** Focused Study, 25 min. **Subject / mode:** shelf with books or jars / Life. **Fallback:** frontal photo with repeated items and shelf edges. **Exercise:** draw shelf direction and first/last placements; compare gaps and widths, not just objects; correct one interval. **Optional:** compare spacing in a row of keys or utensils. **Revisit:** composition 19–24; perspective 39. **Read More:** why repeated gaps can make useful comparison units; distinguish equal-looking spacing from equal physical spacing under perspective. **Sources:** E12 PDF pp. 180–211; WG18 pp. 50–71; N99 pp. 91–116 (repeated divisions). **Placement:** transfers proportion checking from one object to relations among several. **Visual:** `cd-m12-repeated-intervals`.

### 13. Compare two directions — angle plus length

**Cluster:** angle and comparative proportion, 13–16. **Primary / reinforced:** angle and length comparison / contour, unit measurement. **Prerequisites:** 07, 10. **Type/time:** Standard, 45 min. **Subject / mode:** folded plain sheet or blank-page booklet seen obliquely / Life. **Fallback:** same-view photo, page edges visible. **Exercise:** compare long axis, short axis and one corner angle; test page alignment; build a broad outline, then correct the most visible relationship. **Optional:** repeat with a mailer or tablet, preserving angle checks. **Revisit:** perspective 34–41; interior 42. **Read More:** explain sighting as comparative observation rather than copying a tool angle as a formula; note viewpoint dependence. **Sources:** WG18 pp. 50–71; E12 PDF pp. 180–211; D85 pp. 70–101. **Placement:** combines two checks on a broad planar subject before depth-heavy work. **Visual:** `cd-m13-angle-and-length`.

### 14. Measure an irregular manufactured object

**Cluster:** angle and comparative proportion, 13–16. **Primary / reinforced:** proportion through a broad envelope / axis, angles, correction. **Prerequisites:** 10–13. **Type/time:** Standard, 45 min. **Subject / mode:** small stapler, tape dispenser or handheld tool / Life. **Fallback:** single three-quarter reference photo. **Exercise:** place the bounding height/width; compare one diagonal and two component sizes; refine around large parts and correct against the real silhouette. **Optional:** choose a different personal object with a similarly clear envelope. **Revisit:** construction 30–33 and self-selected 85–93. **Read More:** discuss block-in as flexible hypothesis; explain how internal subdivisions can be checked relative to the whole. **Sources:** P16 pp. 26–29, 57–63; A11 Ch. 3, “The Block In” and “Measuring”; WG18 pp. 50–71. **Placement:** gives measurement transfer beyond boxes, plants and vessels. **Visual:** `cd-m14-tool-measurement`.

### 15. Look into the opening — simple foreshortening

**Cluster:** angle and comparative proportion, 13–16. **Primary / reinforced:** foreshortened appearance and apparent length / axis, proportion, overlap and observation. **Prerequisites:** 10, 13. **Type/time:** Standard, 45 min. **Subject / mode:** spoon viewed from near its handle end / Life. **Fallback:** straight-on photo from that viewpoint. **Exercise:** Compare the object's major axis, visible outer shape, near/far lengths and overlap; draw the broad observed silhouette and correct it from the same view. Use the spoon/flashlight as a simple projection example; do not require ellipse construction here. **Optional:** turn the spoon a small amount and thumbnail only the changed proportions. **Revisit:** perspective 40; hand/figure foreshortening 58, 63. **Read More:** explain foreshortening as changed projection rather than physical shortening; distinguish this early visual comparison from the ellipse construction taught later in 27. **Sources:** WG18 pp. 50–71; D85 pp. 128–147; N99 pp. 117–154. **Placement:** introduces apparent shortening with simple observed shapes while reserving ellipse construction for its focused cluster. **Visual:** `cd-m15-simple-foreshortening`.

### 16. Compare a group — whole arrangement proportion

**Cluster:** angle and comparative proportion, 13–16. **Primary / reinforced:** relational proportion across a group / measurement, negative space, composition. **Prerequisites:** 10–15. **Type/time:** Standard, 45 min. **Subject / mode:** tin, small carton and folded cloth arranged with overlaps / Life. **Fallback:** matching three-object reference photo. **Exercise:** mark group envelope and page placement; compare top/side margins, overlaps, and one object-to-object ratio; correct the arrangement before detail. **Optional:** remove one object and recheck the group silhouette. **Revisit:** composition 18–25; extended 17 and 25. **Read More:** explain that relational accuracy includes spacing and overlap, not just each object's size; connect local checking to whole-image read. **Sources:** D85 pp. 70–101, 172–197; WG18 pp. 168–188; GW14 pp. 58–70. **Placement:** consolidates single-object checks in a recognizable group. **Visual:** `cd-m16-group-proportion`.

### 17. Extended Study — measured arrangement from two checks

**Cluster:** Module 3 consolidation. **Primary / reinforced:** measurement, proportion, angles, alignment, negative space. **Prerequisites:** 10–16. **Type/time:** Extended Study, 75 min. **Subject / mode:** learner's choice of personal object group with one planar and one rounded form / Life. **Fallback:** ArtApp three-object photo with clear overlaps. **Exercise:** 10-minute placement; 45-minute drawing with one unit check and one angle check; step back, correct; compare against a second criterion. **Optional:** 15-minute redraw of one object from memory, then compare to the subject. **Revisits:** consolidates 10–16; applies 03–04, 06–09. **Read More:** why a small set of measurements can coordinate the whole drawing and when measurement itself needs revising. **Sources:** WG18 pp. 50–71; E12 PDF pp. 180–211; P16 pp. 26–29, 98–104. **Placement:** gives measurement enough mileage before composition studies. **Visual:** `cd-m17-extended-measurement`.

### 18. Three crops, three readings — crop and scale

**Cluster:** frame and negative shape, 18–20. **Primary / reinforced:** cropping and scale / page placement, negative space. **Prerequisites:** 16. **Type/time:** Standard, 45 min. **Subject / mode:** mug and book from life at one stable viewpoint / Life. **Fallback:** photo framed at the same viewpoint; learner draws thumbnails, not the photo itself. **Exercise:** Reuse the pilot crop comparison on one fixed mug-and-book arrangement: draw three small frames with different margins, compare subject scale and which edges exit, choose one crop and make a checked drawing. Keep the same objects and view while the frame changes. **Optional:** repeat thumbnails for a shoe or personal object. **Revisit:** composition 19–25 and later thumbnails/integrated studies 81–93. **Read More:** explain the frame as an active boundary and compare edge-entry/crop choices; avoid rules-of-thirds as a universal requirement. **Sources:** GW14 pp. 58–70; P16 pp. 81–95; D85 pp. 172–197. **Placement:** moves the pilot crop material into the focused composition cluster after measurement and merges the duplicated three-crop task. **Visual:** `cd-m18-three-crops`. **Existing pilot visuals:** lesson-06-crop-and-negative-shapes, lesson-06-two-frame-warmup, lesson-06-crop-thumbnails-exercise and lesson-06-tangent-mistake (unchanged).

### 19. Balance the spaces between objects

**Cluster:** frame and negative shape, 18–20. **Primary / reinforced:** balance, negative space and visual weight / proportion, overlap. **Prerequisites:** 06, 16, 18. **Type/time:** Standard, 45 min. **Subject / mode:** folded cloth beside a box / Life. **Fallback:** overhead/three-quarter photo, no dramatic perspective. **Exercise:** thumbnail the outside shape and two interior gaps; move one object and redraw only the gaps; compare page balance before choosing. **Optional:** test a narrower frame with the same setup. **Revisit:** composition 22–25 and all extended studies. **Read More:** distinguish perceptual balance from symmetry; discuss how mass, spacing, crop and contrast can alter visual weight without a formula. **Sources:** GW14 pp. 14–70; D85 pp. 172–197; Garvey-Williams page reference has photography examples; transfer decisions to drawn thumbnails. **Placement:** makes composition a relational problem before discussing focal point. **Visual:** `cd-m19-negative-space-balance`.

### 20. Choose what overlaps — arrange for depth

**Cluster:** frame and negative shape, 18–20. **Primary / reinforced:** overlap as compositional and depth choice / silhouette, negative space, form. **Prerequisites:** 08, 16. **Type/time:** Standard, 45 min. **Subject / mode:** sneaker, small parcel and rolled sock / Life. **Fallback:** supplied fixed-view photo of the same group with clear overlap. **Exercise:** compare two placements, draw group envelope and gaps, then choose which contour interruptions best explain front/middle/back; keep laces and package marks out of the drawing. **Optional:** reverse two objects and thumbnail the change. **Revisit:** perspective/depth 34–42; composition 25, 51, 80. **Read More:** explain overlap as both space cue and composition choice; note overlap alone does not prove scale/distance. **Sources:** D85 pp. 128–147, 172–197; P16 pp. 81–95; A11 Ch. 2 “Design”. **Placement:** uses a different familiar setup from Lesson 16 so the learner can transfer the group/spacing skill to a new subject while testing contour interruption. **Visual:** `cd-m20-overlap-composition`.

### 21. Find and fix a tangency

### 80. Extended Study — natural form with cloth

**Cluster:** Module 10 consolidation. **Primary / reinforced:** irregular form, group composition, value, texture, edge and cloth. **Prerequisites:** 73–79. **Type/time:** Extended Study, 90 min. **Subject / mode:** shell or rough stone on folded cloth / Life. **Fallback:** ArtApp-owned still-life photo with visible fabric support. **Exercise:** 12-minute thumbnails; 55-minute drawing; midpoint compare main silhouette, cloth support and light groups; correct one relationship, then add selective marks. **Optional:** 15-minute cropped detail of a fold/object overlap. **Revisits:** consolidates 73–79; applies measurement, construction, value, edge/composition. **Read More:** explain how to preserve a hierarchy across dissimilar materials and how to avoid letting texture erase the main form. **Sources:** H09 pp. 218–231; WG18 pp. 168–188; D85 pp. 102–127, 148–171; P16 pp. 81–95. **Placement:** gathers the course's main forms of evidence before independent work. **Visual:** `cd-m80-extended-organic-cloth`.

### 81. Return to your baseline — spaced observation check

**Cluster:** recall and reference literacy, 81–85. **Primary / reinforced:** retrieval, comparison and self-diagnosis / observation, proportion, line. **Prerequisites:** 01. **Type/time:** Focused Study, 25 min. **Subject / mode:** same mug or a similar mug at the Lesson 01 viewpoint / Life. **Fallback:** if Lesson 01 was not saved, use the supplied mug photo and compare the redraw with the brief memory sketch. **Exercise:** make a brief memory sketch, then an observed redraw; compare with the saved Lesson 01 drawing if available, or with the memory sketch, and name one strategy change rather than only quality. **Optional:** repeat with another L1–9 subject of the learner's choice. **Revisits:** explicit baseline from 01; all observation/measurement/correction. **Read More:** explain spaced return and retrieval as a memory cue; do not present it as a proven drawing intervention or as a score. **Sources:** WG18 pp. 15–23, 50–71; D85 pp. 10–39; Picard pp. 43–44. **Placement:** a deliberate same-object repeat makes progress and remaining questions visible. **Visual:** `cd-m81-baseline-return`.

### 82. Life and photograph — compare the view

**Cluster:** recall and reference literacy, 81–85. **Primary / reinforced:** life-versus-photo comparison / viewpoint, value, crop, depth, movement. **Prerequisites:** 34, 43. **Type/time:** Standard, 45 min. **Subject / mode:** same small still life observed directly and in a self-made phone photo / Both intentionally used. **Fallback:** paired ArtApp-owned photo and setup photo if learner lacks camera; compare the real arrangement first if possible. **Exercise:** make two small drawings of the same scene; list one projection/crop/value/edge difference observed, then decide what both sources preserve. **Optional:** photograph at a second distance and compare perspective. **Revisits:** photo selection in 83–85; all source-based lessons. **Read More:** teach that photographs are chosen projections/records: lens position/distance alter perspective, crop removes evidence, exposure may compress/clamp values, and time stops movement; effects are photograph-dependent. **Sources:** Picard pp. 18–20; E12 PDF pp. 180–211, 251–289. **Placement:** learners have enough drawing experience to notice what each source gives and omits. **Visual:** `cd-m82-life-photo-comparison`.

### 83. Audit a photograph before drawing

**Cluster:** recall and reference literacy, 81–85. **Primary / reinforced:** photographic-reference literacy / crop, viewpoint, value, motion. **Prerequisite:** 82. **Type/time:** Standard, 45 min. **Subject / mode:** supplied adult gesture photograph with a visible crop/light issue / Ref. **Fallback:** second supplied pose photo with a different issue. **Exercise:** inspect viewpoint, crop, clipped highlights/shadows, frozen movement and pose legibility; choose the more useful source; make a short gesture/shape study and annotate one limitation. **Optional:** improve the crop without retouching the source. **Revisits:** pose 52–58 and all later reference drawings. **Read More:** provide a practical reference checklist and qualify that lens, display and lighting effects are not present in every photo; close selfies exaggerate near/far relationships through viewpoint. **Sources:** Picard pp. 18–20; H09 pp. 3–28; E12 PDF pp. 180–211. **Placement:** makes source selection a drawing decision rather than an afterthought. **Visual:** `cd-m83-photo-audit`.

### 84. A limited window or outdoor view

**Cluster:** recall and reference literacy, 81–85. **Primary / reinforced:** integrated depth and large-shape observation / composition, perspective, value grouping. **Prerequisites:** 18, 34, 43. **Type/time:** Standard, 45 min. **Subject / mode:** view from a window or one nearby outdoor scene / Either. **Fallback:** ArtApp-owned simple local view with clear foreground/middle/background. **Exercise:** choose a crop; group 3 spatial layers and 3 values; draw large shapes, compare one alignment/overlap, correct one depth cue; no landscape rendering study. **Optional:** change crop or remove one layer in a thumbnail. **Revisits:** perspective 41–42; composition 18–25; final choice. **Read More:** explain how scale, overlap, placement and value suggest depth; mark this as a short application, not landscape-specific instruction. **Sources:** P16 pp. 81–95, 106–113; D85 pp. 128–147; N99 pp. 1–116. **Placement:** preserves broad experience with environment while leaving advanced landscape to its own course. **Visual:** `cd-m84-limited-view`.

### 85. Turn the subject — viewpoint transfer

**Cluster:** recall and reference literacy, 81–85. **Primary / reinforced:** transfer construction/measurement across viewpoints / proportion, ellipse, perspective, observation. **Prerequisites:** 26–28, 34–36. **Type/time:** Standard, 45 min. **Subject / mode:** learner-selected boxy or rounded household object turned to a new view / Life. **Fallback:** paired ArtApp-owned photos of the same object from two views. **Exercise:** draw one view, rotate or select second view, compare axis/rim/overlap and silhouette; construct lightly and correct to each view. **Optional:** draw only two changed negative spaces. **Revisits:** 27, 31, 26–41; transfer and correction. **Read More:** explain what construction can carry between views and what must be re-observed; viewpoint changes projection and visible contours. **Sources:** WG18 pp. 50–71; D85 pp. 128–147; N99 pp. 117–154; A11 Ch. 3. **Placement:** tests structural understanding without an unfamiliar subject. **Visual:** `cd-m85-viewpoint-transfer`.

### 86. Simplify a complex subject

**Cluster:** integrated studies, 86–88. **Primary / reinforced:** selective simplification / composition, shape, value, mark economy. **Prerequisites:** 22–23, 43, 65. **Type/time:** Standard, 45 min. **Subject / mode:** bicycle section, desk lamp or other visually complex household item / Either. **Fallback:** supplied three-quarter photo with clear large parts. **Exercise:** thumbnail the largest shapes and value masses; draw only the structures needed to identify it; add detail to one focal area; correct one overcomplicated region. **Optional:** reduce the drawing to 5 shapes. **Revisits:** 22–24, 31–33, 71–72. **Read More:** explain simplification as preserving relationships and hierarchy rather than deleting detail at random; note that construction and observation remain paired. **Sources:** GW14 pp. 114–159; D85 pp. 148–171, 172–197; LC12 pp. 81–96, 177–187. **Placement:** challenges the learner to make an editing plan before drawing. **Visual:** `cd-m86-complex-simplification`.

### 87. Arrange a subject around a chosen focal relationship

**Cluster:** integrated studies, 86–88. **Primary / reinforced:** composition, crop and hierarchy / proportion, negative shape, value. **Prerequisites:** 18, 22–23. **Type/time:** Standard, 45 min. **Subject / mode:** learner-arranged objects, including one personally meaningful item / Life. **Fallback:** supplied varied-object still-life photo. **Exercise:** choose the relationship to emphasize; make two thumbnails with different crop/scale; complete selected drawing and check whether the intended relationship reads. **Optional:** ask another viewer which area they notice first (optional feedback, not required). **Revisits:** composition throughout; practice reviewing evidence. **Read More:** explain how intention can guide selection while visible evidence still constrains the drawing; visual hierarchy may have several valid solutions. **Sources:** GW14 pp. 8–12, 58–70, 114–159; P16 pp. 81–95; D85 pp. 172–197. **Placement:** learner begins selecting the problem, not only the subject. **Visual:** `cd-m87-chosen-composition`.

### 88. People in a small space — integrated figure/setting

**Cluster:** integrated studies, 86–88. **Primary / reinforced:** figure placement within space / gesture, scale, perspective, composition. **Prerequisites:** 34, 37, 52, 54, 56. **Type/time:** Standard, 45 min. **Subject / mode:** adult seated or standing in a simple room reference / Ref. **Fallback:** supplied full figure plus room photo pair, or self-reference in mirror cropped against a plain background. **Exercise:** place figure silhouette against room; compare head/seat/floor contacts and scale; draw setting only as much as needed to support pose. **Optional:** crop tighter and redraw the figure placement thumbnail. **Revisits:** gesture 52–58, room 42, composition. **Read More:** explain scale and overlap as relations between figure and setting; do not require anatomy or elaborate perspective. **Sources:** H09 pp. 29–55; N99 pp. 67–116; P16 pp. 98–105. **Placement:** gives figure drawing an environmental application before capstones. **Visual:** `cd-m88-person-in-space`.

### 89. Extended Study — draw a familiar interior independently

**Cluster:** sustained transfer, 89–92. **Primary / reinforced:** observation, composition, measurement, perspective, value, correction. **Prerequisites:** 18, 22, 34–37, 43. **Type/time:** Extended Study, 90 min. **Subject / mode:** learner's own familiar room corner / Life. **Fallback:** supplied interior photo with one main piece of furniture. **Exercise:** plan view/crop, draw large planes and object placements, use one perspective check and one value grouping, pause mid-way and revise one relation. **Optional:** 15-minute alternate crop. **Revisits:** 34–42, 18–25, 26–33, 43–51. **Read More:** explain independent sequencing (view → large relationships → chosen checks → value → review) and how to omit irrelevant detail. **Sources:** N99 pp. 1–116; P16 pp. 45–55, 81–95, 98–104; D85 pp. 128–147. **Placement:** sustained transfer provides a near-capstone test with familiar subject. **Visual:** `cd-m89-independent-interior`.

### 90. Choose a question, then choose a subject

**Cluster:** sustained transfer, 89–92. **Primary / reinforced:** self-directed practice planning / retrieval, deliberate attention, subject transfer. **Prerequisites:** none; choose one concept question and a subject that makes it visible. **Type/time:** Standard, 45 min. **Subject / mode:** learner selects a subject matching one named skill question / Either. **Fallback:** manifest photo recommendation for the chosen subject family. **Exercise:** state one drawing question (e.g. ellipse, gap, cast shadow), choose a subject that makes it visible, do a focused 30-minute drawing and complete a compare/correct note. **Optional:** repeat that question on a different subject. **Revisits:** all major concepts, chosen by learner. **Read More:** explain alignment of practice target, subject and visible success criterion; keep the selection frame simple. **Sources:** Picard pp. 43–44; D85 pp. 10–39. **Placement:** shifts some sequencing responsibility to learner without turning the lesson into an unstructured project. **Visual:** `cd-m90-practice-choice`.

### 91. Diagnose before you polish

**Cluster:** sustained transfer, 89–92. **Primary / reinforced:** error diagnosis and targeted correction / proportion, value, edge, composition. **Prerequisites:** none; no earlier lesson is a gate. **Type/time:** Standard, 45 min. **Subject / mode:** learner selects one earlier drawing and a fresh simple object for a corrective redraw / Either. **Fallback:** supplied plain object photo for new drawing; older drawing is learner's own. **Exercise:** use a short review card (placement, proportion, shape, depth/light, edge); choose one priority, make a new 20-minute drawing, and compare that one criterion. **Optional:** repeat using a different criterion only if useful. **Revisits:** self-correction throughout course. **Read More:** explain task-specific feedback, how to identify one actionable mismatch, and why vague self-judgment is less useful than visible evidence. **Sources:** D85 pp. 10–39; P16 pp. 26–29, 43–44. **Placement:** makes independent error correction explicit before final course study. **Visual:** `cd-m91-diagnose-correct`.

### 92. Open study — draw, pause, redraw

**Cluster:** sustained transfer, 89–92. **Primary / reinforced:** sustained observation and independent correction / learner-selected concepts. **Prerequisites:** none; choose one familiar skill to apply and self-check. **Type/time:** Standard, 45 min. **Subject / mode:** learner selects one personally engaging subject / Either. **Fallback:** choose from ArtApp-owned object, portrait, gesture, interior or natural-form reference library. **Exercise:** 5-minute plan; 30-minute drawing; 5-minute break/step-back; 5-minute redrawing or targeted correction. **Optional:** keep a short next-time question. **Revisits:** learner selects two previous concepts. **Read More:** explain productive sustained attention, planned breaks and targeted redrawing; distinguish practice record from a finished-art evaluation. **Sources:** Picard pp. 43–44; D85 pp. 10–39; one applicable locator from the chosen concept (for example D85 pp. 70–101 for measurement, D85 pp. 172–197 for composition, N99 pp. 1–116 for space, or D85 pp. 102–127 for light). **Placement:** tests learner choice and transfer with a modest scaffold. **Visual:** `cd-m92-open-study`.

### 93. Independent Core Drawing — choose and review

**Cluster:** final integration. **Primary / reinforced:** full drawing cycle and independent application / observation, composition, measurement, form/space, value, edge, correction. **Prerequisites:** none; any prior concept may be selected, without a particular lesson gate. **Type/time:** Extended Study, 120 min maximum target, flexible stop point. **Subject / mode:** learner chooses a real subject or useful photo, ideally one that combines at least three familiar questions / Either. **Fallback:** choose one supplied ArtApp reference pack if a real setup is difficult. **Exercise:** 15-minute plan/thumbnails; 70-minute study; pause; correct one priority; finish with self-review and two next steps. **Optional:** a separate later repeat on a different subject, not a multi-day polished assignment. **Revisits:** consolidates 90–92 planning, open study and correction; integrates only the earlier concepts the learner selects. **Read More:** explain independent planning, choice of scale, review, limits and next learning route; do not introduce a new theory module. **Sources:** D85 pp. 10–39 and Picard pp. 43–44 for observation/practice planning; add the exact earlier source locator for the selected concept (for example D85 pp. 70–101 for measurement, N99 pp. 1–116 for space, or D85 pp. 102–127 for light). **Placement:** completes the process without testing polish, speed or talent. **Visual:** `cd-m93-independent-core-study`.
