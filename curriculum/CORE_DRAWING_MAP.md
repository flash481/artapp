# Core Drawing — 100-lesson production map

**Planning only.** This is not lesson copy. Do not replace or rewrite `curriculum/lessons/lesson-01.json` through `lesson-09.json`, and do not generate the planned assets in this phase. The mapped content after lesson 09 is a production specification to review and approve.

**Field convention:** every lesson lists module/cluster, primary and reinforced concepts, true prerequisites, type/duration, subject, life/reference mode, fallback, exercise, optional concept-specific practice, deliberate revisits, Read More purpose, source/further-reading locators, placement reason, and visual IDs. Expanded visual prompts, source-image candidates, and fallback photo briefs are in `CORE_DRAWING_VISUAL_MANIFEST.md`. The complete bibliographic key follows the map.

**Types and time:** Focused Study = 25 min; Standard Lesson = 45 min; Extended Study = the row-specific 75–120 min. Targets include cue/setup, drawing, comparison and review; they are not all drawing time. In a Focused Study, keep cue/setup brief (about 4–5 min total), include any warm-up in the exercise, and reserve time to compare/correct. Optional reinforcement adds 10–20 min and is never a progression gate. Extended studies introduce little or no major new theory, consolidate the named recent lessons and include a pause to compare and correct.
**Mode vocabulary:** Life = Life recommended; Ref = Reference recommended; Either = Either equally suitable; Both = Both intentionally used. Every Life row names its subject, a low-friction setup and a supplied-photo fallback; the visual manifest gives the photo/setup brief. For human subjects, a live model is optional; supplied references and self/mirror alternatives remain available.

## Module 1 — Early exposure and observation cycle (Lessons 01–09)

The pilot is intentionally retained in its existing order and detailed content. Lesson-map fields below reconcile it with the new structure; visual IDs refer to existing assets. No text or visual in the enriched lesson files is rewritten by this proposal.

### 01. Your first drawing — compare and correct

**Cluster:** first observation cycle. **Primary / reinforced:** observation and self-review / contour, shape. **Prerequisites:** none. **Type/time:** existing Standard Lesson, 45 min. **Subject / mode:** mug from life / Life. **Fallback:** keep the same mug photo from the learner's viewpoint. **Exercise:** complete the existing two-pass baseline and name one visible relation to improve. **Optional:** repeat with a different household object, correcting only the selected relation. **Revisit:** observation/correction opens every later extended study and returns explicitly at 88–100. **Read More:** existing deep dive on perceptual errors, comparison and one-action correction; do not rewrite. **Sources / further reading:** D85 pp. 10–39; WG18 pp. 15–23. **Placement:** sets the process and leaves a private baseline. **Planned fallback visual:** cd-l01-mug-reference. **Visuals:** existing `lesson-01-observation-cycle`, `lesson-01-warmup-check-one-relation`, `lesson-01-two-pass-mug-study`.

### 02. Follow an edge — contour

**Cluster:** first observation cycle. **Primary / reinforced:** contour and edge direction / observation, correction. **Prerequisite:** 01. **Type/time:** existing Standard, 45 min. **Subject / mode:** shoe from life / Life. **Fallback:** ArtApp-owned side-view shoe photo, same crop. **Exercise:** trace slow contour turns, then make a second checked pass using the existing lesson. **Optional:** change to a tool or folded cloth and mark only contour changes. **Revisit:** line and edge selection in 10–15 and 72–78. **Read More:** existing deep dive on contour as attention practice, not an outline recipe; keep the distinction between observed edge and remembered symbol. **Sources:** WG18 pp. 24–37; E12 PDF pp. 121–154. **Placement:** gives the learner a narrow observational task immediately after baseline. **Planned fallback visual:** cd-l02-shoe-reference. **Visuals:** existing `lesson-02-contour-turns`, `lesson-02-warmup-edge-checks`, `lesson-02-two-contour-passes`.

### 03. Shapes around a plant — negative space

## Module 2 — Shape, edge and correction (Lessons 10–16)

These linked lessons concentrate on observed shapes and the correction cycle. They do not add a separate flower/petal topic.

### 10. The object you see, not the object you name — observation checkpoints

**Cluster:** shape comparison, 10–12. **Primary / reinforced:** observation and comparison / contour, proportion, correction. **Prerequisites:** 01–05. **Type/time:** Focused Study, 25 min. **Subject / mode:** small desk radio or calculator / Life. **Fallback:** straight-on ArtApp-owned photo. **Exercise:** 2-minute cue/setup, 3-minute memory outline, 10-minute observed drawing, 5-minute mismatch/correction, and 5-minute compare/review; total 25 minutes. **Optional:** repeat with a personal object and the same one-check protocol. **Revisit:** explicit compare/correct in every later module. **Read More:** explain labels/symbols vs visible relations; why a criterion makes self-check usable; distinguish this practice prompt from a brain-hemisphere claim. **Sources:** D85 pp. 10–39; WG18 pp. 15–23; E12 PDF pp. 76–98 (attention exercise, not its lateralization explanation). **Placement:** turns exposure into a repeatable method on a different subject than the mug. **Visual:** `cd-m10-observation-checkpoints`.

### 11. Contour turns and pauses — contour with checks

**Cluster:** shape comparison, 10–12. **Primary / reinforced:** contour direction and line continuity / observation, line economy. **Prerequisite:** 02. **Type/time:** Focused Study, 25 min. **Subject / mode:** spoon or peeler / Life. **Fallback:** single side-view photo. **Exercise:** mark six direction changes, draw one slow contour pass, compare the tip/bowl/handle angles, then correct two turns. **Optional:** do a five-turn contour of a shoe, changing subject but keeping the criterion. **Revisit:** 15, 72–74. **Read More:** explain contour as a chosen boundary; where contour disappears into value/overlap; when looking away helps and when frequent checks help. **Sources:** WG18 pp. 24–37; E12 PDF pp. 121–154; P16 pp. 31–41. **Placement:** deepens the pilot contour task without repeating its shoe subject. **Visual:** `cd-m11-contour-turns`.

### 12. One clear silhouette — big shapes before parts

**Cluster:** shape comparison, 10–12. **Primary / reinforced:** silhouette and internal shape grouping / contour, negative space. **Prerequisite:** 03. **Type/time:** Focused Study, 25 min. **Subject / mode:** compact parcel with tape or a simple toy / Life. **Fallback:** single-view neutral photo. **Exercise:** block the whole outside shape, mark two interior divisions, cover details, compare width/height/tilt, then correct the silhouette. **Optional:** use a packaged food item; preserve the whole-to-part sequence. **Revisit:** measurement block-in 17–23; construction 33–39. **Read More:** clarify why a silhouette is a useful first test but cannot describe every interior feature; connect positive and negative shape without a rigid recipe. **Sources:** WG18 pp. 24–37, 50–71; A11 Ch. 3, “The Block In”; BWZ18 pp. 6–9 (scaffold only). **Placement:** makes broad form recognizable before measurement detail. **Visual:** `cd-m12-silhouette-first`.

### 13. Gaps in a chair — negative shapes as checks

**Cluster:** spatial relations, 13–15. **Primary / reinforced:** negative space / contour, angles, proportion. **Prerequisite:** 03. **Type/time:** Standard, 45 min. **Subject / mode:** dining chair / Life. **Fallback:** straight-on chair photo. **Exercise:** draw page placement and two openings first; use their angles/widths to place seat, legs and back; compare/correct one gap. **Optional:** test one chair opening against a different chair, no full redraw. **Revisit:** 25–27; later profile/figure gaps at 63–70. **Read More:** explain shared borders and visual grouping; caution that the gap is evidence, not an abstract shape replacing the chair. **Sources:** E12 PDF pp. 155–179; P16 pp. 26–29; GW14 pp. 14–36. **Placement:** transfers negative-space looking to a structured subject with multiple openings. **Visual:** `cd-m13-chair-gaps`.

### 14. Compare the slant — angles and alignments

**Cluster:** spatial relations, 13–15. **Primary / reinforced:** angle comparison and plumb/level alignment / measurement, observation. **Prerequisites:** 04, 10. **Type/time:** Standard, 45 min. **Subject / mode:** scissors and ruler arranged at a slight angle / Life. **Fallback:** top-down photo with both objects fully visible. **Exercise:** mark three dominant axes; compare their tilt to the page and to one another; test two alignments with an unmarked pencil; redraw the largest error. **Optional:** check one slant and one vertical on an appliance. **Revisit:** 17–23, 41–48, figure gesture 59–65. **Read More:** distinguish measured direction from decorative diagonal; explain plumb/level only relative to the chosen view. **Sources:** WG18 pp. 50–71; E12 PDF pp. 180–211; P16 pp. 26–29. **Placement:** isolates direction before proportion studies become more demanding. **Visual:** `cd-m14-angle-alignments`.

### 15. Where forms meet — overlaps and interrupted edges

**Cluster:** spatial relations, 13–15. **Primary / reinforced:** overlap order and edge interruption / contour, negative shape, form. **Prerequisites:** 08, 13. **Type/time:** Standard, 45 min. **Subject / mode:** glass jar behind a small carton / Life. **Fallback:** ArtApp-owned photo at the same view. **Exercise:** place the large shapes, identify which contour stops at each overlap, draw the visible relationships and one through-line lightly, then correct a tangency. **Optional:** change which object is in front and sketch the altered overlap. **Revisit:** 25–32; construction 37–40; still life 58, 87. **Read More:** explain T-junctions/occlusion as cues, distinguish a visible contour from an inferred hidden edge. **Sources:** D85 pp. 128–147; P16 pp. 57–63; A11 Ch. 5, “Understanding Form”. **Placement:** teaches depth through observation before formal perspective. **Visual:** `cd-m15-overlap-order`.

### 16. Extended Study — arrange, compare, correct

**Cluster:** Module 2 consolidation. **Primary / reinforced:** observation, silhouette, contour, negative shape, correction. **Prerequisites:** 10–15. **Type/time:** Extended Study, 75 min. **Subject / mode:** three learner-chosen household objects with one boxy, one rounded and one optional soft form, plus four supplied gesture references as a brief warm-up / Both intentionally used. **Fallback:** use the supplied three-object photo if setup is inconvenient; the gesture reference pack includes eight individual full-size poses. **Exercise:** 5-minute setup/cue; 4-minute gesture preview (four poses, one minute each); 10-minute thumbnails; 46-minute object drawing including a midpoint compare/correct pause; 10-minute review/notes. Total: 75 minutes. Reuse pose set cd-m59-gesture-pose-set. **Optional:** 10-minute second crop of the same arrangement. **Revisits:** consolidates 10–15; applies 01–09. **Read More:** why sustained looking reveals relational errors; how to choose one correction rather than erase everything; the pose warm-up is a first glimpse, not a gesture prerequisite. **Sources:** D85 pp. 10–39, 70–101; WG18 pp. 50–71; P16 pp. 98–104 (staged-study structure). **Placement:** first sustained transfer study follows two focused clusters. **Visual:** `cd-m16-extended-correction-study`.

## Module 3 — Comparative measurement (Lessons 17–24)

### 17. Choose a measuring unit — relative size

**Cluster:** unit and overall proportion, 17–19. **Primary / reinforced:** comparative measurement / observation, page placement. **Prerequisite:** 04. **Type/time:** Focused Study, 25 min. **Subject / mode:** shoebox or food package / Life. **Fallback:** orthogonal-view photo with entire object visible. **Exercise:** choose one visible width as a provisional unit; compare height and depth; check the result against the whole silhouette and revise. **Optional:** use a different package, choose a new unit. **Revisit:** 18–24; every constructed object and figure. **Read More:** ratios are comparisons within a view, not absolute numbers; explain that the initial unit is a convenience and can be rechecked. **Sources:** WG18 pp. 50–71; D85 pp. 70–101; P16 pp. 26–29. **Placement:** introduces the simplest proportional measuring tool on a boxy, stable subject. **Visual:** `cd-m17-unit-measure`.

### 18. Check the whole before the parts — height, width and placement

**Cluster:** unit and overall proportion, 17–19. **Primary / reinforced:** overall proportion / measurement, silhouette, composition. **Prerequisite:** 17. **Type/time:** Focused Study, 25 min. **Subject / mode:** potato or egg / Life. **Fallback:** one pear photo at eye height. **Exercise:** place top/bottom/left/right; compare overall height to width; only then add the stem and one interior change. **Optional:** compare an egg and pear using separate units. **Revisit:** extended 24; organic forms 80–83. **Read More:** explain envelope-first drawing and why local detail can hide a whole-shape error; clarify that natural variation defeats idealized geometric templates. **Sources:** WG18 pp. 50–71; D85 pp. 70–101; A11 Ch. 3, “Measuring”. **Placement:** applies the unit method to an irregular natural object. **Visual:** `cd-m18-whole-proportion`.

### 19. Repeated intervals — compare spacing

**Cluster:** unit and overall proportion, 17–19. **Primary / reinforced:** interval and spacing / negative shape, alignment. **Prerequisite:** 13. **Type/time:** Focused Study, 25 min. **Subject / mode:** shelf with books or jars / Life. **Fallback:** frontal photo with repeated items and shelf edges. **Exercise:** draw shelf direction and first/last placements; compare gaps and widths, not just objects; correct one interval. **Optional:** compare spacing in a row of keys or utensils. **Revisit:** composition 26–31; perspective 46. **Read More:** why repeated gaps can make useful comparison units; distinguish equal-looking spacing from equal physical spacing under perspective. **Sources:** E12 PDF pp. 180–211; WG18 pp. 50–71; N99 pp. 91–116 (repeated divisions). **Placement:** transfers proportion checking from one object to relations among several. **Visual:** `cd-m19-repeated-intervals`.

### 20. Compare two directions — angle plus length

**Cluster:** angle and comparative proportion, 20–23. **Primary / reinforced:** angle and length comparison / contour, unit measurement. **Prerequisites:** 14, 17. **Type/time:** Standard, 45 min. **Subject / mode:** folded plain sheet or blank-page booklet seen obliquely / Life. **Fallback:** same-view photo, page edges visible. **Exercise:** compare long axis, short axis and one corner angle; test page alignment; build a broad outline, then correct the most visible relationship. **Optional:** repeat with a mailer or tablet, preserving angle checks. **Revisit:** perspective 41–48; interior 49. **Read More:** explain sighting as comparative observation rather than copying a tool angle as a formula; note viewpoint dependence. **Sources:** WG18 pp. 50–71; E12 PDF pp. 180–211; D85 pp. 70–101. **Placement:** combines two checks on a broad planar subject before depth-heavy work. **Visual:** `cd-m20-angle-and-length`.

### 21. Measure an irregular manufactured object

**Cluster:** angle and comparative proportion, 20–23. **Primary / reinforced:** proportion through a broad envelope / axis, angles, correction. **Prerequisites:** 17–20. **Type/time:** Standard, 45 min. **Subject / mode:** small stapler, tape dispenser or handheld tool / Life. **Fallback:** single three-quarter reference photo. **Exercise:** place the bounding height/width; compare one diagonal and two component sizes; refine around large parts and correct against the real silhouette. **Optional:** choose a different personal object with a similarly clear envelope. **Revisit:** construction 37–40 and self-selected 92–100. **Read More:** discuss block-in as flexible hypothesis; explain how internal subdivisions can be checked relative to the whole. **Sources:** P16 pp. 26–29, 57–63; A11 Ch. 3, “The Block In” and “Measuring”; WG18 pp. 50–71. **Placement:** gives measurement transfer beyond boxes, plants and vessels. **Visual:** `cd-m21-tool-measurement`.

### 22. Look into the opening — simple foreshortening

**Cluster:** angle and comparative proportion, 20–23. **Primary / reinforced:** foreshortening and apparent length / ellipse exposure, axis, proportion. **Prerequisites:** 09, 17. **Type/time:** Standard, 45 min. **Subject / mode:** spoon viewed from near its handle end / Life. **Fallback:** straight-on photo from that viewpoint. **Exercise:** compare apparent bowl width and handle length, mark the axis, draw the broad shape and check the overlap of parts. **Optional:** turn the spoon a small amount and thumbnail only the changed proportions. **Revisit:** perspective 47; hand/figure foreshortening 65, 70. **Read More:** explain foreshortening as changed projection rather than physical shortening; do not apply an ideal ellipse if the observed opening differs. **Sources:** WG18 pp. 38–71; D85 pp. 128–147; N99 pp. 117–154. **Placement:** connects the ellipse pilot to an object with stronger near/far differences. **Visual:** `cd-m22-simple-foreshortening`.

### 23. Compare a group — whole arrangement proportion

**Cluster:** angle and comparative proportion, 20–23. **Primary / reinforced:** relational proportion across a group / measurement, negative space, composition. **Prerequisites:** 17–22. **Type/time:** Standard, 45 min. **Subject / mode:** tin, spoon and plain carton arranged with overlaps / Life. **Fallback:** matching three-object reference photo. **Exercise:** mark group envelope and page placement; compare top/side margins, overlaps, and one object-to-object ratio; correct the arrangement before detail. **Optional:** remove one object and recheck the group silhouette. **Revisit:** composition 25–32; extended 24 and 32. **Read More:** explain that relational accuracy includes spacing and overlap, not just each object's size; connect local checking to whole-image read. **Sources:** D85 pp. 70–101, 172–197; WG18 pp. 168–188; GW14 pp. 58–70. **Placement:** consolidates single-object checks in a recognizable group. **Visual:** `cd-m23-group-proportion`.

### 24. Extended Study — measured arrangement from two checks

**Cluster:** Module 3 consolidation. **Primary / reinforced:** measurement, proportion, angles, alignment, negative space. **Prerequisites:** 17–23. **Type/time:** Extended Study, 75 min. **Subject / mode:** learner's choice of personal object group with one planar and one rounded form / Life. **Fallback:** ArtApp three-object photo with clear overlaps. **Exercise:** 10-minute placement; 45-minute drawing with one unit check and one angle check; step back, correct; compare against a second criterion. **Optional:** 15-minute redraw of one object from memory, then compare to the subject. **Revisits:** consolidates 17–23; applies 10–16. **Read More:** why a small set of measurements can coordinate the whole drawing and when measurement itself needs revising. **Sources:** WG18 pp. 50–71; E12 PDF pp. 180–211; P16 pp. 26–29, 98–104. **Placement:** gives measurement enough mileage before composition studies. **Visual:** `cd-m24-extended-measurement`.

## Module 4 — Composition as active choice (Lessons 25–32)

### 25. Three crops, three readings — crop and scale

**Cluster:** frame and negative shape, 25–27. **Primary / reinforced:** cropping and scale / page placement, negative space. **Prerequisites:** 06, 23. **Type/time:** Standard, 45 min. **Subject / mode:** shoe at one stable viewpoint / Either. **Fallback:** crop the supplied view for each thumbnail; then choose either life or photo for the larger study. **Exercise:** draw three small frames with different margins; compare the subject's scale and which edges exit; choose one and make a checked drawing. **Optional:** repeat thumbnails for a personal object. **Revisit:** each later composition/extended study. **Read More:** explain the frame as an active boundary and compare edge-entry/crop choices; avoid rules-of-thirds as a universal requirement. **Sources:** GW14 pp. 58–70; P16 pp. 81–95; D85 pp. 172–197. **Placement:** returns to the pilot's crop skill with a new subject and explicit alternatives. **Visual:** `cd-m25-three-crops`.

### 26. Balance the spaces between objects

**Cluster:** frame and negative shape, 25–27. **Primary / reinforced:** balance, negative space and visual weight / proportion, overlap. **Prerequisites:** 13, 23, 25. **Type/time:** Standard, 45 min. **Subject / mode:** folded cloth beside a box / Life. **Fallback:** overhead/three-quarter photo, no dramatic perspective. **Exercise:** thumbnail the outside shape and two interior gaps; move one object and redraw only the gaps; compare page balance before choosing. **Optional:** test a narrower frame with the same setup. **Revisit:** composition 29–32 and all extended studies. **Read More:** distinguish perceptual balance from symmetry; discuss how mass, spacing, crop and contrast can alter visual weight without a formula. **Sources:** GW14 pp. 14–70; D85 pp. 172–197; Garvey-Williams page reference has photography examples; transfer decisions to drawn thumbnails. **Placement:** makes composition a relational problem before discussing focal point. **Visual:** `cd-m26-negative-space-balance`.

### 27. Choose what overlaps — arrange for depth

**Cluster:** frame and negative shape, 25–27. **Primary / reinforced:** overlap as compositional and depth choice / silhouette, negative space, form. **Prerequisites:** 15, 23. **Type/time:** Standard, 45 min. **Subject / mode:** tin, soap carton and folded cloth / Life. **Fallback:** supplied photo with clear overlap. **Exercise:** compare two placements, draw group envelope and gaps, then choose which contour interruptions best explain front/middle/back. **Optional:** reverse two objects and thumbnail the change. **Revisit:** perspective/depth 41–49; composition 32, 58, 87. **Read More:** explain overlap as both space cue and composition choice; note overlap alone does not prove scale/distance. **Sources:** D85 pp. 128–147, 172–197; P16 pp. 81–95; A11 Ch. 2 “Design”. **Placement:** uses setup adjustment to make the lesson's concept visible. **Visual:** `cd-m27-overlap-composition`.

### 28. Find and fix a tangency

**Cluster:** focus and simplification, 28–31. **Primary / reinforced:** tangencies and edge relationships / overlap, crop, negative shape. **Prerequisites:** 15, 25. **Type/time:** Standard, 45 min. **Subject / mode:** chair leg near table edge, photographed or life arrangement / Either. **Fallback:** supplied chair/table reference. **Exercise:** thumbnail the near-touching edges; shift crop or placement slightly; select the version with clearer overlap/spacing, not the one following an absolute ban. **Optional:** find one ambiguity in an earlier drawing and make a thumbnail correction. **Revisit:** every extended study and edge group 72–74. **Read More:** explain why nearly touching contours can merge or confuse, and when deliberate contact is useful; avoid treating tangencies as a law. **Sources:** D85 pp. 172–197; GW14 pp. 72–101; P16 pp. 81–95. **Placement:** introduces a common ambiguity as a solvable decision, not a rule list. **Visual:** `cd-m28-tangency-correction`.

### 29. Choose a focal shape — visual hierarchy

**Cluster:** recall and reference literacy, 88–92. **Primary / reinforced:** photographic-reference literacy / crop, viewpoint, value, motion. **Prerequisite:** 89. **Type/time:** Standard, 45 min. **Subject / mode:** supplied adult gesture photograph with a visible crop/light issue / Ref. **Fallback:** second supplied pose photo with a different issue. **Exercise:** inspect viewpoint, crop, clipped highlights/shadows, frozen movement and pose legibility; choose the more useful source; make a short gesture/shape study and annotate one limitation. **Optional:** improve the crop without retouching the source. **Revisits:** pose 59–65 and all later reference drawings. **Read More:** provide a practical reference checklist and qualify that lens, display and lighting effects are not present in every photo; close selfies exaggerate near/far relationships through viewpoint. **Sources:** Picard pp. 18–20; H09 pp. 3–28; E12 PDF pp. 180–211. **Placement:** makes source selection a drawing decision rather than an afterthought. **Visual:** `cd-m90-photo-audit`.

### 91. A limited window or outdoor view

**Cluster:** recall and reference literacy, 88–92. **Primary / reinforced:** integrated depth and large-shape observation / composition, perspective, value grouping. **Prerequisites:** 25, 41 and 50. **Type/time:** Standard, 45 min. **Subject / mode:** view from a window or one nearby outdoor scene / Either. **Fallback:** ArtApp-owned simple local view with clear foreground/middle/background. **Exercise:** choose a crop; group 3 spatial layers and 3 values; draw large shapes, compare one alignment/overlap, correct one depth cue; no landscape rendering study. **Optional:** change crop or remove one layer in a thumbnail. **Revisits:** perspective 48–49; composition 25–32; final choice. **Read More:** explain how scale, overlap, placement and value suggest depth; mark this as a short application, not landscape-specific instruction. **Sources:** P16 pp. 81–95, 106–113; D85 pp. 128–147; N99 pp. 1–116. **Placement:** preserves broad experience with environment while leaving advanced landscape to its own course. **Visual:** `cd-m91-limited-view`.

### 92. Turn the subject — viewpoint transfer

**Cluster:** recall and reference literacy, 88–92. **Primary / reinforced:** transfer construction/measurement across viewpoints / proportion, ellipse, perspective, observation. **Prerequisites:** 09, 33–35 and 41–43. **Type/time:** Standard, 45 min. **Subject / mode:** learner-selected boxy or rounded household object turned to a new view / Life. **Fallback:** paired ArtApp-owned photos of the same object from two views. **Exercise:** draw one view, rotate or select second view, compare axis/rim/overlap and silhouette; construct lightly and correct to each view. **Optional:** draw only two changed negative spaces. **Revisits:** 08–09, 33–48; transfer and correction. **Read More:** explain what construction can carry between views and what must be re-observed; viewpoint changes projection and visible contours. **Sources:** WG18 pp. 38–71; D85 pp. 128–147; N99 pp. 117–154; A11 Ch. 3. **Placement:** tests structural understanding without an unfamiliar subject. **Visual:** `cd-m92-viewpoint-transfer`.

### 93. Simplify a complex subject

**Cluster:** integrated studies, 93–95. **Primary / reinforced:** selective simplification / composition, shape, value, mark economy. **Prerequisites:** 29 or 30, 50 and 72. **Type/time:** Standard, 45 min. **Subject / mode:** bicycle section, desk lamp or other visually complex household item / Either. **Fallback:** supplied three-quarter photo with clear large parts. **Exercise:** thumbnail the largest shapes and value masses; draw only the structures needed to identify it; add detail to one focal area; correct one overcomplicated region. **Optional:** reduce the drawing to 5 shapes. **Revisits:** 29–31, 38–40, 78–79. **Read More:** explain simplification as preserving relationships and hierarchy rather than deleting detail at random; note that construction and observation remain paired. **Sources:** GW14 pp. 114–159; D85 pp. 148–171, 172–197; LC12 pp. 81–96, 177–187. **Placement:** challenges the learner to make an editing plan before drawing. **Visual:** `cd-m93-complex-simplification`.

### 94. Arrange a subject around a chosen focal relationship

**Cluster:** integrated studies, 93–95. **Primary / reinforced:** composition, crop and hierarchy / proportion, negative shape, value. **Prerequisites:** 06, 25 and 29 or 30. **Type/time:** Standard, 45 min. **Subject / mode:** learner-arranged objects, including one personally meaningful item / Life. **Fallback:** supplied varied-object still-life photo. **Exercise:** choose the relationship to emphasize; make two thumbnails with different crop/scale; complete selected drawing and check whether the intended relationship reads. **Optional:** ask another viewer which area they notice first (optional feedback, not required). **Revisits:** composition throughout; practice reviewing evidence. **Read More:** explain how intention can guide selection while visible evidence still constrains the drawing; visual hierarchy may have several valid solutions. **Sources:** GW14 pp. 8–12, 58–70, 114–159; P16 pp. 81–95; D85 pp. 172–197. **Placement:** learner begins selecting the problem, not only the subject. **Visual:** `cd-m94-chosen-composition`.

### 95. People in a small space — integrated figure/setting

**Cluster:** integrated studies, 93–95. **Primary / reinforced:** figure placement within space / gesture, scale, perspective, composition. **Prerequisites:** 59, 61 and 63, plus 41 or 44 for room context. **Type/time:** Standard, 45 min. **Subject / mode:** adult seated or standing in a simple room reference / Ref. **Fallback:** supplied full figure plus room photo pair, or self-reference in mirror cropped against a plain background. **Exercise:** place figure silhouette against room; compare head/seat/floor contacts and scale; draw setting only as much as needed to support pose. **Optional:** crop tighter and redraw the figure placement thumbnail. **Revisits:** gesture 59–65, room 49, composition. **Read More:** explain scale and overlap as relations between figure and setting; do not require anatomy or elaborate perspective. **Sources:** H09 pp. 29–55; N99 pp. 67–116; P16 pp. 98–105. **Placement:** gives figure drawing an environmental application before capstones. **Visual:** `cd-m95-person-in-space`.

### 96. Extended Study — draw a familiar interior independently

**Cluster:** sustained transfer, 96–100. **Primary / reinforced:** observation, composition, measurement, perspective, value, correction. **Prerequisites:** 25 or 29, 41–44 and 50; prior extended studies are recommended practice, not gates. **Type/time:** Extended Study, 90 min. **Subject / mode:** learner's own familiar room corner / Life. **Fallback:** supplied interior photo with one main piece of furniture. **Exercise:** plan view/crop, draw large planes and object placements, use one perspective check and one value grouping, pause mid-way and revise one relation. **Optional:** 15-minute alternate crop. **Revisits:** 41–49, 25–32, 33–40, 50–58. **Read More:** explain independent sequencing (view → large relationships → chosen checks → value → review) and how to omit irrelevant detail. **Sources:** N99 pp. 1–116; P16 pp. 45–55, 81–95, 98–104; D85 pp. 128–147. **Placement:** sustained transfer provides a near-capstone test with familiar subject. **Visual:** `cd-m96-independent-interior`.

### 97. Choose a question, then choose a subject

**Cluster:** sustained transfer, 96–100. **Primary / reinforced:** self-directed practice planning / retrieval, deliberate attention, subject transfer. **Prerequisites:** none; choose one concept question and a subject that makes it visible. **Type/time:** Standard, 45 min. **Subject / mode:** learner selects a subject matching one named skill question / Either. **Fallback:** manifest photo recommendation for the chosen subject family. **Exercise:** state one drawing question (e.g. ellipse, gap, cast shadow), choose a subject that makes it visible, do a focused 30-minute drawing and complete a compare/correct note. **Optional:** repeat that question on a different subject. **Revisits:** all major concepts, chosen by learner. **Read More:** explain alignment of practice target, subject and visible success criterion; keep the selection frame simple. **Sources:** Picard pp. 43–44; D85 pp. 10–39. **Placement:** shifts some sequencing responsibility to learner without turning the lesson into an unstructured project. **Visual:** `cd-m97-practice-choice`.

### 98. Diagnose before you polish

**Cluster:** sustained transfer, 96–100. **Primary / reinforced:** error diagnosis and targeted correction / proportion, value, edge, composition. **Prerequisites:** one earlier drawing and one fresh simple subject; no particular lesson is required. **Type/time:** Standard, 45 min. **Subject / mode:** learner selects one earlier drawing and a fresh simple object for a corrective redraw / Either. **Fallback:** supplied plain object photo for new drawing; older drawing is learner's own. **Exercise:** use a short review card (placement, proportion, shape, depth/light, edge); choose one priority, make a new 20-minute drawing, and compare that one criterion. **Optional:** repeat using a different criterion only if useful. **Revisits:** self-correction throughout course. **Read More:** explain task-specific feedback, how to identify one actionable mismatch, and why vague self-judgment is less useful than visible evidence. **Sources:** D85 pp. 10–39; P16 pp. 26–29, 43–44. **Placement:** makes independent error correction explicit before final course study. **Visual:** `cd-m98-diagnose-correct`.

### 99. Open study — draw, pause, redraw

**Cluster:** sustained transfer, 96–100. **Primary / reinforced:** sustained observation and independent correction / learner-selected concepts. **Prerequisites:** none; choose one familiar skill to apply and self-check. **Type/time:** Standard, 45 min. **Subject / mode:** learner selects one personally engaging subject / Either. **Fallback:** choose from ArtApp-owned object, portrait, gesture, interior or natural-form reference library. **Exercise:** 5-minute plan; 30-minute drawing; 5-minute break/step-back; 5-minute redrawing or targeted correction. **Optional:** keep a short next-time question. **Revisits:** learner selects two previous concepts. **Read More:** explain productive sustained attention, planned breaks and targeted redrawing; distinguish practice record from a finished-art evaluation. **Sources:** Picard pp. 43–44; D85 pp. 10–39; one applicable locator from the chosen concept (for example D85 pp. 70–101 for measurement, D85 pp. 172–197 for composition, N99 pp. 1–116 for space, or D85 pp. 102–127 for light). **Placement:** tests learner choice and transfer with a modest scaffold. **Visual:** `cd-m99-open-study`.

### 100. Independent Core Drawing — choose and review

**Cluster:** final integration. **Primary / reinforced:** full drawing cycle and independent application / observation, composition, measurement, form/space, value, edge, correction. **Prerequisites:** none; any prior concept may be selected, without a particular lesson gate. **Type/time:** Extended Study, 120 min maximum target, flexible stop point. **Subject / mode:** learner chooses a real subject or useful photo, ideally one that combines at least three familiar questions / Either. **Fallback:** choose one supplied ArtApp reference pack if a real setup is difficult. **Exercise:** 15-minute plan/thumbnails; 70-minute study; pause; correct one priority; finish with self-review and two next steps. **Optional:** a separate later repeat on a different subject, not a multi-day polished assignment. **Revisits:** consolidates 97–99 planning, open study and correction; integrates only the earlier concepts the learner selects. **Read More:** explain independent planning, choice of scale, review, limits and next learning route; do not introduce a new theory module. **Sources:** D85 pp. 10–39 and Picard pp. 43–44 for observation/practice planning; add the exact earlier source locator for the selected concept (for example D85 pp. 70–101 for measurement, N99 pp. 1–116 for space, or D85 pp. 102–127 for light). **Placement:** completes the process without testing polish, speed or talent. **Visual:** `cd-m100-independent-core-study`.
