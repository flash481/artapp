# Phase 2B curriculum decisions

This is the lead architect's decision record. The 11-book Phase 2A corpus and page conventions are described in `sources/research/RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md`; the [source mapping](source-mapping.json) assigns all 70 indexed source concept records a final status and lesson placement. The mapping's `MERGED` status means that a useful idea appears across sources and is taught once in an integrated progression, not that every source sentence is reproduced. Source counts are evidence of located teaching material, not evidence that one method produces better outcomes.

## One course, not 11 summaries

Observation, comparison, and revision begin with a mug and shoe. Contour, negative space, and measurement offer concrete ways to look more accurately (Dodson, printed pp. 10–39 and 70–101; Edwards, PDF pp. 121–211; Watson Garcia, printed pp. 15–71). Structural tools enter promptly as aids to that looking: simple volumes and ellipses with real cups, then formal convergence in rooms and books (Picard, printed pp. 45–63; Norling, printed pp. 1–66, 117–154). The course keeps observational depth cues and geometric projection as complementary ways to solve different spatial problems. Perspective returns in landscape, interior, figure-in-room, and a rotated character; it is never a long isolated block.

Line and gesture answer different questions. Contour investigates a boundary; gesture captures direction and weight. Both later support form and composition (Picard, printed pp. 31–41; Hampton, printed pp. 4–49). Sighting and measured ratios are checks against an observed subject, not substitutes for it. Head-ball axes from Loomis (printed pp. 22–35) are useful for rotation; Edwards's observed profile (PDF pp. 227–240) and Watson Garcia's frontal study (printed pp. 120–137) keep construction answerable to an individual sitter. Facial ratios remain adjustable hypotheses.

Value begins with three groups, then light/shadow families and cast shadow, flower/plant light, distance, portrait planes, charcoal masses, and independent tonal design (Dodson, printed pp. 102–127; Aristides, EPUB Chapters Four–Five and Chapter Ten Lesson One; Picard, printed pp. 65–79; Loomis *Creative Illustration*, printed pp. 81–96). Edge and texture choices are taught as selective visual decisions, not exhaustive rendering (Dodson, printed pp. 148–171). Composition starts in lesson 06 with crops and continues through still-life hierarchy, landscape thumbnails, tangencies, value design, narrative arrangement, and learner-chosen projects (Dodson, printed pp. 172–197; Picard, printed pp. 81–95; Garvey-Williams, printed pp. 14–70 and 114–159). No geometric ratio is imposed as a universal composition rule.

The 72-lesson length allows an early grasp of all central strands, repeated subject changes, and seven integration projects without padding long runs of boxes or spheres. Lesson 24 is the first sustained still life. Lessons 36, 48, 59, 60, 68, and 72 integrate increasingly independent work. Lesson 71 makes the final project's research, thumbnails, medium choice, value plan, and focal decision visible before the final drawing.

## Subjects and media

The source corpus does not contain a substantial dedicated flower-drawing method. The course therefore uses flowers only for three required applications—measurement (04), overlapping form (16), and light (34)—plus an optional learner choice in lesson 66. Other organic studies use a plant, leafy branch, shell, tree, or natural object. Lesson 59 is a **natural forms** project, not a botanical specialism. This reduced emphasis reflects both the source evidence and the learner's Phase 2B correction.

Landscape has direct staged support mainly from Picard's three-value mountain and sugar-palm studies (printed pp. 65–70, 106–113), with compositional support from Garvey-Williams and the general depth, value, and perspective sources. The course returns to real views and accessible places rather than inventing a comprehensive landscape method from thin source coverage. Water in lesson 51 is a bounded application of value and reflection, not a claim that the corpus contains a full water-drawing chapter.

The sampled Pokémon book offers directional guides, large shapes, correction, and detail (printed pp. 6–9, 74, 106, 138–139). Four stylised exercises use those ideas as transfer: initial shape analysis, new view, new action, and original creature design. The first three allow a learner-chosen Pokémon reference for personal study; no licensed character art is planned as a public asset. Figure work proceeds from Hampton's gesture and major masses (printed pp. 4–85), through selective landmarks and hands, into a person-in-space and portrait project. Detailed muscle memorisation stays optional. Heads appear after a substantial foundation and before the last section.

Graphite is the low-friction starting medium. Charcoal enters at lesson 30 for broad masses, then returns in landscape atmosphere, portrait light, gesture, edges, and portrait interpretation. Later projects allow a medium choice that serves the drawing. The corpus's wash, pastel, Conté, pen, colour finishing, and painting material is outside this graphite/charcoal core. The two source concept records primarily about colour finishing are explicitly `OMITTED` in the source mapping; medium-handling lessons retain only relevant graphite/charcoal practice.

## Methods retained as alternatives or selective studies

- Edwards's attention-reset tasks, including negative space and upside-down looking, can be useful alternate ways to examine an edge. The global left/right-brain learner-type explanation is excluded (Edwards, PDF pp. 57–98; `sources/research/TARGETED_EXTERNAL_RESEARCH.md`).
- Observed face intervals, Loomis's turning head construction, and Hampton's simplified head construction serve different checks. No one template is mandatory.
- Master study is analytical and limited to two lessons: compare line or value in an authentic, lawfully available work, then transfer the finding to an original observed subject (Aristides, EPUB Chapter Six and Chapter Ten Lesson Two; Dodson, printed pp. 40–49). The Phase 2A master index provides leads, not a cleared asset. Phase 2C must verify exact artwork, collection, URL, date, and rights before using a work.
- Norling's advanced shadows, reflections, unusual viewpoints, and mechanical projection remain optional extensions rather than a full engineering-perspective syllabus (printed pp. 155–200). Drapery and detailed figure anatomy also stay selective.

## Deliberate omissions and qualifications

- **Out of scope:** colour theory/painting, extensive pastel/Conté/wash sampling, professional illustration market workflow, exhaustive anatomy, and scientific botanical illustration. These do not advance the present graphite/charcoal sketchbook course enough to displace drawing time.
- **Unsupported universal claims:** fixed 10,000 hours to mastery (Picard, printed p. 43); Golden Ratio/Fibonacci as universal nature or beauty laws (Aristides, EPUB Chapter Two; Picard, printed pp. 90–93); globally left/right-brained learner types (Edwards, PDF pp. 57–75); and guaranteed viewer responses or colour emotions (Watson Garcia, printed pp. 170–175; Garvey-Williams, printed pp. 106–110). Keep useful exercises as testable prompts without those claims. See `sources/research/GAPS_AND_DISAGREEMENTS.md` and `TARGETED_EXTERNAL_RESEARCH.md`.
- **Contextual shorthand:** Norling's horizon/eye-level equation applies to level-ground viewpoint construction, not every visible skyline; closely spaced vanishing points can create strong distortion without being universally “incorrect” (printed pp. 4–10, 49). Face ratios are adjusted to the sitter (Watson Garcia, printed pp. 120–124).
- **Dated categories:** Loomis's racial-skull typology and prescriptive beauty/fashion standards are excluded (printed pp. 77, 90). Teach individual observation and construction instead.
- **Source illustrations:** five map entries list private illustration candidates for targeted review. Their inclusion is not an asset approval. Geometry should normally be redrawn as original SVG; selected artwork requires a separate rights and provenance check. The static password cannot protect private book images.

## Evidence limits carried forward

Dodson, Hampton, Loomis *Creative Illustration*, and Pokémon were analyzed from scan samples; Pokémon's four inspected lessons do not represent all 70. Aristides uses EPUB chapter/section anchors rather than fixed pages. The source files provide descriptions of methods, not comparative teaching trials. Phase 2C should use `lesson-map.json` → private `QUICK_REFERENCE` → relevant analysis → a targeted original-page check only when a decision turns on detail.
