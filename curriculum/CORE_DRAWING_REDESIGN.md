# Core Drawing — final 150-lesson architecture and audit

**Status:** This is the final planning record for the canonical 150-lesson architecture in [CORE_DRAWING_MAP.md](CORE_DRAWING_MAP.md). The learner approved the broad architecture and requested this targeted Landscape & Nature expansion; the expanded map has now passed prerequisite, subject-coverage, landscape false-coverage, and specialist-creep reviews. The course is content-complete for Core Drawing. No lesson JSON, teaching image, or app functionality was authored or changed in this pass. Pilot Lessons 01–09 remain unchanged prototypes/reference material.

## 1. Scope, method, and evidence limits

This was a targeted expansion of the approved 130-lesson architecture, not a wholesale redesign. It adds six early outdoor transfers and a 14-lesson Landscape & Nature foundation. No existing lesson was removed. Lesson 117 was sharpened as an explicit tree-structure transfer in its existing slot; the rest of the established teaching strands remain in sequence, renumbered where insertions required it.

Audits distinguish explicit practice from a subject name. A practice counts as explicit when the learner draws a recognizable form or scene, compares it with observed evidence, and makes a choice, diagnosis, or correction. A later return counts when it uses the skill in another context or with less guidance. The audit measures planned opportunities, not learner outcomes. No adult learning trial has tested this sequence.

The learner-provided source tree was checked and contained no books, extracts, or book analyses. The private domain note at `sources/research/landscape-core-domain.md` records a targeted scan of museum collection/teaching resources and historic drawing manuals. Those sources support observation, selection, structural forms, selective marks, reflection study, and short outdoor recording as examples of practice. Museum activities often target children; Ruskin is historically situated; neither source group demonstrates an empirically optimal adult lesson sequence. The references informed drawing decisions, not claims of pedagogical consensus.

## 2. Why the course now has 150 lessons

The previous 130-lesson architecture already covered the general drawing foundations. The outdoor world was the remaining meaningful transfer gap. Six earlier practice lessons and fourteen focused lessons address it without dropping or compressing the existing strands. The resulting 150 follows actual practice needs; it is not a quota or ceiling.

- Six new spiral encounters add **210 minutes** (3 hours 30 minutes): four 30-minute Focused Studies and two 45-minute Standard Lessons.
- The 14-lesson Landscape & Nature module adds **675 minutes** (11 hours 15 minutes): thirteen 45-minute lessons and one 90-minute Extended Study.
- Net-new course time is **885 minutes** (14 hours 45 minutes). Lesson 117 also makes its existing 45-minute branching exercise explicitly serve tree structure, but that is not additional course time.
- The whole course totals **6,990 minutes (116 hours 30 minutes)**: 13 Focused Studies at 30 minutes, 126 Standard Lessons at 45 minutes, and 11 Extended Studies (four at 75 minutes, seven at 90 minutes). This includes setup, drawing, comparison, correction, and review.
- Landscape/nature practice comprises 20 new lessons plus the broadened Lesson 117: **930 minutes (15 hours 30 minutes), or 13.3%** of the total. This is a substantial foundation while keeping the course broad.
- Figure, portrait, hands/feet, and clothing practice remains 34 lessons and **1,635 minutes (27 hours 15 minutes; 23.4%)** of the course.

The eleven Extended Studies are Lessons 14, 35, 50, 64, 76, 88, 105, 121, 138, 147, and 150. The guided landscape study is deliberately 90 minutes, with a complete-scene capability checkpoint at minute 60.

## 4. Every new landscape/nature lesson

### Early spiral encounters

| Lesson | Title | Transfer point |
|---:|---|---|
| 07 | Read a tree as a silhouette and a set of gaps | Silhouette and branch-gap check immediately after the first envelope-shape lesson; previews but does not replace negative-space work in 08. |
| 24 | Crop a simple outdoor view | Applies framing and page boundaries to sky/land, a dominant silhouette, and a useful path, wall, or tree group. |
| 37 | Build a rock from mass and planes | Applies the newly taught plane/mass construction to an irregular outdoor form. |
| 59 | Read distance along a path or boundary | Uses scale, overlap, intervals, and placement on an outdoor path, fence, wall, field edge, or stream. |
| 77 | Group a tree mass against sky | Transfers newly taught graphite value grouping to a tree/sky or wooded-edge relationship. |
| 94 | Choose edges across outdoor distance | Applies hard/quiet edges, contrast, and mark economy to near and distant vegetation. |

Lesson 117, **Follow branching forms from trunk to major branches**, stays in the existing natural-form sequence. Its exercise now makes trunk axis/taper, branching hierarchy, attachment, overlap, and asymmetry an explicit bridge to the tree lessons.

### Landscape & Nature foundations: Lessons 125–138

| Lesson | Title | Explicit drawing work |
|---:|---|---|
| 125 | Edit a landscape into a few connected shapes | Compare crops; identify dominant silhouettes, sky/land division, connected masses, direction, focal area, useful contrast/edge, and what can disappear. |
| 126 | Establish foreground, middle ground and distance | Use overlap, relative scale, vertical placement, receding intervals, and observed convergence without forcing every scene into three bands. |
| 127 | Read terrain and ground planes | Draw a slope, hill, bank, or path as turning ground; include a simple wall, fence, road, or path beside natural ground/vegetation and observe both together. |
| 128 | Construct a tree from trunk to canopy | Study axis, taper, branch direction/attachment/hierarchy, overlap, asymmetry, silhouette, negative gaps, and canopy masses before texture. |
| 129 | Group foliage as canopy masses | Build canopy volume and light/shadow groups; select outer edges, internal gaps, branch visibility, leaf-cluster marks, and a detail focus. |
| 130 | Organize a tree group or woodland edge | Compare one connected mass with overlapping near/far groups, rhythms, silhouettes, and negative gaps. |
| 131 | Construct rocks and irregular outdoor masses | Begin with mass/orientation, planes, overlap, contact and light/shadow; use only cracks or texture that explain form and scale. |
| 132 | Organize landscape values before detail | Make a limited-value thumbnail for visible sky, land, trees, buildings, foreground shadows, or water; correct broad relations before texture. |
| 133 | Show atmospheric distance through observation | Compare equal treatment with observed differences in value range, edge, detail, and shape complexity. Weather, lighting, distance, and exposure can vary the cues. |
| 134 | Draw sky and clouds as landscape masses | Treat sky as a compositional shape; preserve paper white, group clouds, and vary soft/defined edges without symbols or meteorological detail. |
| 135 | Observe still and gently moving water | Draw the water plane and broad reflected shapes/value first; compare distortion, broken reflection, edge, and surface interruption before restrained ripples. |
| 136 | Suggest simple moving water | Observe flow, surface planes, disrupted reflections, bank/rock interaction, and directional marks; avoid memorized stripes. |
| 137 | Make quick outdoor sketches as conditions change | Produce a 3-minute thumbnail, a 5–10-minute visual note, and a short study for different purposes; establish light/shadow early and simplify or omit changing forms. |
| 138 | Extended Study — compose and complete a landscape | Draw a built form with surrounding natural forms. Finish a readable whole scene, limited value plan, and one comparison-led correction within 60 minutes; use the final 30 minutes for selective refinement/review. Water returns if visible. |

### Landscape study duration

Lesson 138 is scheduled for 90 minutes. Its capability check is explicit: by minute 60 the learner has completed a readable landscape containing the selected built/natural scene, crop, major spatial shapes, limited value plan, and one observation-led correction. The final 30 minutes permit selected edge/detail work, a review, and a deliberate stopping choice. Thus the course rehearses a complete 45–60-minute-scale landscape task inside a longer guided study rather than claiming that only a thumbnail is enough.

## 6. Prerequisite and order audit

The sequence audit found no hard prerequisite-order violation. The following developmental chain is explicit:

- Shape → tree silhouette: 06 → 07; the branch-gap preview in 07 is followed immediately by general negative-space instruction in 08.
- Composition → outdoor crop/editing: 23 → 24 → 125.
- Form/mass → irregular rock: 36 → 37 → 131.
- Observed depth → outdoor boundary → landscape layers/terrain: 51–58 → 59 → 126–127.
- Graphite/value → tree against sky → landscape value: 65–76 → 77 → 132.
- Edge/texture → near/far vegetation → foliage economy/atmosphere: 89–93 → 94 → 129–133.
- Organic structure → tree branching → full tree/canopy → foliage → groups: 115–117 → 128 → 129 → 130.
- Still/gently moving water/reflection → moving-water flow: 135 → 136; use either later when the selected view contains water.
- Guided outdoor selection and practice → integrated 60-minute scene checkpoint → learner-led transfer: 125–137 → 138 → 142–150.

The previous draft treated too many later topics as prerequisites. The map now limits Lesson 125 to relevant prior composition, spatial, value, edge, and organic-form practice; Lesson 137 requires only crop/depth/value tools and labels topic studies as optional; Lesson 138 names core scene tools and makes feature-specific studies conditional; Lesson 148 does not require unrelated room/landscape work; Lesson 149 treats all subject studies as optional routes rather than gates. Prerequisites in the map indicate useful preparation, never a mastery barrier.

## 7. Landscape false-coverage audit and resolution

An independent audit found that all requested landscape capabilities had explicit exercises, with two low-severity gaps in the first draft: built/natural integration was optional, and the full landscape exercise did not define the requested 45–60-minute success check. Both are resolved in the current map:

| Finding | Final resolution |
|---|---|
| Built forms could be omitted from every outdoor exercise. | L127 now requires a simple wall/fence/road/path alongside ground or vegetation; L138 requires a built form among surrounding natural forms. Both ArtApp-owned fallbacks must show the combination. |
| A 90-minute study alone did not verify a complete shorter landscape session. | L138 requires a readable whole scene, spatial structure, limited value plan, and one correction by minute 60; the remaining time is selective refinement and review. |
| Water was named but could become a generic optional mark-making exercise. | L135 begins with reflected shape/value and surface interruption; L136 studies flow and broken reflections. Both are dedicated, required Core lessons; recurrence at 138 and 149–150 is conditional on the selected view. |
| Atmospheric distance could become a mechanical lighten/blur rule. | L133 compares equal treatment with observed value/edge/detail/shape differences; it instructs the learner to check the view and includes weather/exposure variation. |
| Outdoor access or changeable weather could block completion. | Every outdoor lesson has an ArtApp-owned fixed-view fallback; L137 gives practical strategies for changing light/shadows, wind, clouds, water, people, and cars. |

The reviewer confirmed that the final L127/L138 requirements and fallbacks close the integration/time gaps and found no other remaining landscape capability that lacked practice.

## 8. Whole-course false-coverage and specialist-creep audit

The whole-course check looked for topics that were only named, taught without later use, out of order, over-scaffolded, duplicated, or disproportionately represented. Core retains practical drawing coverage across objects, arrangements, interiors, people, portraits, hands/feet, organic forms, cloth, and landscape. The expansion preserves the full existing 130-lesson sequence and adds only the seven earlier landscape encounters plus the focused 14-lesson foundation (six are new early lessons; Lesson 117 is broadened in place). It does not increase flowers, plant taxonomy, anatomy, meteorology, architecture, color media, or high-finish rendering.

**Specialist boundary:** Core now teaches ordinary tree structure, foliage grouping, terrain, rocks, spatial depth, atmospheric cues, sky/cloud masses, simple still/moving water, a built/natural scene, and outdoor sketch practice. Defer breaking surf/waves, waterfalls, spray/foam, complex transparency or underwater forms, advanced reflections, polished seascapes, complex coastal weather, geology, species-specific botany, and detailed landscape rendering to later study.

The audit found no reason to cut an existing lesson or add another specialist topic. Landscape is 13.3% of planned practice time when the existing Lesson 117 tree-structure transfer is counted, and adds 885 minutes net. Figure/portrait/extremity/clothing practice remains 23.4%. These figures describe planned time, not measured difficulty or enjoyment. A later learner pilot can expose workload or engagement issues without reopening this content-complete architecture by default.

## 9. Source checks, privacy, and visual direction

The private landscape domain note summarizes these targeted sources:

- Getty Museum Education, [*Framing the Landscape*](https://www.getty.edu/education/k-12-learning/framing-the-landscape/), its [teacher guide](https://www.getty.edu/education/for_teachers/curricula/impressionism/lesson02.html), and [*The Changing Landscape*](https://www.getty.edu/education/teachers/classroom_resources/curricula/landscapes/lesson04.html) for framing, tree observation, and multiple depth cues.

- National Gallery of Art material on [Monet’s waterscapes](https://www.nga.gov/educational-resources/sense-place-france/monets-waterscapes) and [Fragonard’s *Mountain Landscape at Sunset*](https://www.nga.gov/research/publications/french-paintings-fifteenth-through-eighteenth-centuries/french-paintings-fifteenth-through-eighteenth-centuries-mountain-landscape-sunset-c-1765) for water observation and rapid, selective landscape studies.
- Ruskin, [*The Elements of Drawing*](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/30325/30325-h/30325-h.htm), §§120–150 and appendix notes, as historically situated advice on trees, rocks, clouds, water, and landscape selection. These passages are not treated as current experimental evidence.

Every eventual reference needs ArtApp ownership or documented rights, stable provenance, a useful alt text, and a fixed-view fallback. Private books, research notes, and source images remain under `sources/` and outside public assets/deployment. Welsh scenes may be chosen for ArtApp-owned references where suitable; no particular location is assumed for the learner.
