# Core Drawing — final architecture review, evidence and depth audit

**Status:** Planning companion for the canonical 130-lesson proposal in CORE_DRAWING_MAP.md. The map replaces the former 93-lesson sequence. No production lesson JSON or final visual was changed or generated in this pass. Existing Lessons 01–09 remain prototype/reference material until architecture approval and later replacement.

## 1. Scope and method

This was a final architecture pass, not a restart of source research. It used the existing private book analyses, source inventory, taxonomy, exercise index, research summary, disagreement/gap notes, and domain reports under sources/. The lead rebuilt lesson placement from a blank Lesson 01, then integrated three independent read-only review passes. Reviewers examined the prior map to identify its defects and the new 130-lesson draft to red-team the result. Findings were accepted when they exposed a real teaching, prerequisite, transfer, or access gap; preferences without a material learning benefit were not converted into extra lessons.

The assessment is architectural, not an empirical claim about measured learner outcomes. Counts below use this coding rule: a dedicated lesson makes the ability an explicit objective; a substantial application requires the learner to draw a recognizable subject, compare it with observed evidence, and correct or diagnose; a revisit requires meaningful later use in a changed context. Categories overlap. Approximate counts are evidence of practice opportunities, not targets.

## 2. Why the sequence has 130 lessons

The count arose from 13 connected modules and their actual drawing jobs:

- Early observation, shape, measurement, and composition need separate guided studies before a learner can make dependable corrections.
- Form receives 14 dedicated lessons, including an explicit bridge into figure masses, rather than asking eight lessons to carry the whole form foundation.
- Space receives 13 dedicated lessons that alternate observed depth cues with formal perspective; object foreshortening is separate and precedes limb and figure applications.
- Graphite tone begins at Lesson 29, before value thumbnails, and then receives 12 deeper lessons with physical technique, light logic, and subject application.
- Human drawing spans gesture, structural figure work, portrait/likeness, hands/feet, clothing, and later integrated studies. These lessons are spaced among other subjects rather than placed in one uninterrupted block.
- Organic construction and drapery each have explicit practice and later transfer. Integration tests independence after the relevant capabilities exist.

The course has 9 Focused Studies, 111 Standard Lessons, and 10 Extended Studies. The 10 extended studies are 13, 33, 47, 60, 72, 83, 99, 115, 127, and 130. Six target 90 minutes; four target 75 minutes. At the mapped durations, required work is about **101 hours 45 minutes**, including setup, drawing, comparison, and review. This is a substantial self-paced course; shortenings should come from later human approval of redundant objectives, not from a numerical ceiling.

**Architecture recommendation:** retain 130 as the proposed sequence for human review. The count follows the separated practice and transfer jobs above; no lesson was added to approach a target. Consider consolidation only where review identifies duplicate learner outcomes, not to return to approximately 100 by arithmetic.

## 5. False-coverage audit and corrections

The prior map sometimes treated a label or a future application as proof of teaching. The final sequence resolves these gaps.

| Prior false or shallow coverage | Final correction |
|---|---|
| Figure proportion had a comparison task but no required drawing | 20 now requires two full-figure block-ins and correction; 75 repeats proportions across body types |
| Gesture/torso masses did not teach limb joints, landmark placement, or mass connections | 76–82 now teach spine, ribcage/pelvis, shoulder/hip relation, observed joints, and connected limb masses |
| Feet appeared as support but had no construction practice | 104–105 teach foot wedge, ankle, heel, toe group, direction, weight, and contact; 107–108 and 127 transfer them |
| The long figure sequence allowed a learner to choose a portrait instead of drawing a full figure | 83 requires a full-body or three-quarter structural study; 127 requires a whole figure in space |
| Generic head construction could be mistaken for likeness | 98 compares two studies of the same sitter; 99 is a sustained recognizable-person portrait; 128 revisits likeness with clothing |
| Foreshortening was named before form/ellipse practice or had only one object example | 58–59 follow ellipses and cylinders; 81–82 teach limbs; 103 teaches hands; 127 requires whole-figure transfer |
| Value-based hierarchy appeared before an explicit tonal method | 29 teaches two-value grouping; 30 side tone/even tone/hatching; 31 gradation/white; 32 then uses value thumbnails |
| Paper care and page smudging arrived after prolonged graphite work | Light pressure, no grooves, gentle correction, and hand/page care are introduced at 01–04 and revisited in 62 and 72 |
| Eraser comparison implied access to multiple tools | 04 and 70 provide one-eraser options; extra erasers are optional |
| A hard/soft lesson requested a lost edge before it had been taught | 85 teaches hard/soft; 86 explicitly introduces lost/found |
| “Correction” sometimes changed the subject, so it did not show whether the diagnosed error was fixed | 45, 83, 98–99, 108, 119–120, and 126 require correction on the same subject/view; new subjects are separately described as transfer |
| A stored Lesson 01 baseline was later assumed but not guaranteed | 01 tells the learner to save/label the drawing; 119 repeats the subject and view |
| Photo fallbacks began before learners were told that a photo fixes crop/viewpoint and records only one view | 01 now gives a short fixed-view/crop/light cue and asks the learner to keep or recreate the setup; 121 expands this into a fuller photo audit |
| “Choose a measuring unit” named comparison but did not show how to sight or transfer it | 14 now demonstrates a steady arm’s-length pencil/thumb unit, a width/feature comparison, and transfer of that ratio to the block-in |
| The first two-value exercise required stable light without a practical setup cue | 29 now asks for a steady one-sided window or lamp, with a fixed-light reference fallback |
| Composition was described as appearing in every drawing, but some lessons had only local technical checks | The blanket claim is removed. Composition has 10 direct lessons and named returns in portraits, rooms, extended studies, and independent planning |
| A person-focused block could become monotonous | Figure practice is spaced: early gesture/proportion 19–20; form bridge 46; structural figure 73–83; material lessons 84–91; heads 92–99; hands/feet 100–108; clothing and integrated applications 118, 127–130 |
| Photo reference could be treated as neutral ground truth | 01 teaches a stable view, crop and light before photo fallbacks; 121 audits crop, viewpoint, exposure, focus and omissions in more depth |
| Late critique did not explicitly include frame/focal read and stopping | 91 and 120 include frame/focal review; 129–130 require a chosen correction and stopping decision |

### Open-ended findings beyond the expected checklist

Reviewers independently found two course-wide risks worth preserving in the design record:

1. **Page-level composition can disappear during a local technical drill.** The final map does not claim that every drawing needs a full composition pass. Extended and integrated studies explicitly revisit frame/focal read; 91 and 120 include it in review.
2. **Source-based sequence does not establish empirical cognitive load or enjoyment.** The plan limits drill-only runs, varies subjects, and uses short focused practice, but learner response during the later pilot remains a genuine review signal. This is a human review question, not a reason to manufacture a new lesson now.

No other independently identified beginner skill required a separate lesson once the photo-audit, baseline, same-subject correction, line/paper care, and integration decisions were explicit.

## 5A. First 20 lesson titles

1. Draw one real thing — look, mark, compare, change
2. Keep the first marks light — pressure and paper
3. Choose point or side — angle, rotation, sharpening
4. Search then select — line and correction
5. Follow an edge — contour direction and turns
6. See the outside shape first
7. Draw the spaces around it
8. Compare slants and alignments
9. Show which form overlaps
10. Divide a large shape into a few parts
11. Repeat and compare the gaps
12. Read a contour through an interruption
13. Extended Study — two objects, one clear drawing
14. Choose a measuring unit
15. Check the whole — height, width and placement
16. Check a long angle and alignment
17. Compare repeated intervals
18. Measure an irregular object
19. Catch a pose in a few lines
20. Compare the pose — proportion and balance

## 5B. Changes from the former 93-lesson architecture

The increase from 93 to 130 is a net count after reordering, splitting overloaded objectives, adding missing practice, and merging duplicate examples. New lesson numbers do not preserve old identities.

| Former sequence area | Decision and current placement |
|---|---|
| L02 bundled pressure, searching and line selection | Split the physical controls into L02 pressure/paper, L03 point/side/angle/rotation, and L04 search/select/correction so each mark is applied immediately. |
| Separate plant-gap and chair-gap examples at L05–06 | Merged into the general negative-shape lesson L07; varied gaps return in 11, 24, 95 and later arrangements instead of repeating near-identical introductions. |
| L10–17 combined unit, whole proportion, intervals, angle/length, irregular measurement and foreshortening | Kept comparative measurement as its own sequence at 14–18, separated object foreshortening into 58–59, and made early pose proportion a drawing task at 20. |

| Eight lessons at L26–33 carried planes, ellipses, cylinders, cross-contour, joins, construction correction and transfer | Expanded and decomposed into L34–47: planes, axis, ellipse, cylinder, rounded/tapered solids, joins, drawing-through, model limits, masses, construction mark control, observed correction, figure bridge and transfer study. |
| Nine lessons at L34–42 combined formal perspective with observed room depth and object foreshortening | Expanded to L48–60. Observed depth cues precede formal one/two-point tools; cylinder/object foreshortening is an explicit separate step and rooms recur without diagram dependence. |

## 10. Independent red-team findings and changes

Three reviewer roles completed independent passes:

- **Complete beginner / graphite reviewer:** flagged pose-proportion comparison without drawing, value thumbnails ahead of first explicit tonal instruction, late paper care, photo literacy arriving after photo fallbacks, under-specified unit sighting, lighting setup, baseline reproducibility, stale lesson-ID references, and ambiguous corrective redraws. The new sequence requires drawings in 20, teaches tone at 29–31 before 32, teaches care in 01–04, introduces fixed-view photo use at 01 before the fuller audit at 121, shows unit sighting/transfer in 14, stabilizes light in 29, and saves or recreates the baseline view.
- **Form / space reviewer:** found the prior eight-form sequence substantively useful but recommended clarifying that an observed near/far relationship—not a construction rule—controls correction and that the late room path must work without formal perspective. The new sequence expands form to 14 lessons, requires observation-led room studies at 60 and 125, uses formal checks only when they resolve a question, qualifies view-dependent foreshortening at 103, and removes the false head/likeness prerequisite from 118.
- **Figure / composition / value reviewer:** identified missing limb landmarks/connections, no foot construction, no guaranteed full-figure study, insufficient direct specific-likeness work, out-of-order lost-edge expectation, optional eraser access, and an overbroad claim that composition appears in every drawing. The new sequence adds explicit landmarks/limbs, feet, required full-figure studies, same-sitter portrait work, ordered edge instruction, one-eraser routes, and calibrated composition claims.
- **Cross-review decisions:** do not add every preferred exercise as a lesson. Use same-subject corrections, practical anatomy, varied bodies and ordinary subjects, optional tools, and later transfer to close gaps without expanding into a separate figure, portrait, or botanical specialist course.

The red-team summary is also in CORE_DRAWING_RED_TEAM.md. Reviewer reports are planning evidence, not approval of production content.
