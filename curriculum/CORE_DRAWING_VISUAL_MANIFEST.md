## Master ArtApp Instructional Visual Style Prompt

> Create an instructional visual for **ArtApp Core Drawing**, a graphite/pencil drawing course for an adult beginner drawing beside a sketchbook. Make it feel like a skilled drawing instructor's working sketchbook on neutral off-white paper: authentic graphite grain, naturally varied pressure, visible but quiet searching/construction marks, darker selected lines over lighter proposals, believable imperfections and occasional correction, restrained hatching/value, and only a few useful hand-lettered annotations. Show the drawing process and the decision the learner needs to make, rather than an immaculate finished illustration. Keep page margins and labels clear on a phone-sized screen; use high contrast for essential marks without turning the image into a polished poster. Match sophistication to course stage: early examples are loose, simple, analytical and visibly corrected; middle examples show more confident construction, line and broad value; later examples handle complex subjects with selective edges/detail and a more resolved study. Vary the composition and mark rhythm so the family feels authored, not templated. Use precise clean diagrams only where geometric accuracy matters, paired with an actual graphite application where useful. No color, charcoal, vector infographic styling, decorative backgrounds, uniform digital strokes, excessive polish, copied book illustrations, fake legible paragraphs, or marks that conceal the teaching point. If the visual includes a photo reference, keep it clearly separate from the pencil demonstration and make the viewpoint/light/crop specified in the lesson believable and stable.

### Photo/setup brief conventions

- **Life setup image (required for every Life lesson and included for the life side of Both lessons):** one ArtApp-owned companion photo or simple line-layout showing the exact mapped object/group, the stable seated viewpoint, and the relationship between learner, table/surface and page. Show the light source when light is part of the lesson. It may be a small inset on the subject-reference card or a separate setup panel; do not make a second complex visual. The per-lesson subject and viewpoint prompt supplies what is arranged. Add no hands or props that distract. A learner should reproduce it in minutes.
- **Mandatory prompt clause for Life/Both rows:** the specific prompt must include a same-view fallback subject reference plus a compact setup inset/panel that makes object placement and viewer position clear; include the page and light where useful. If the setup is already obvious from the subject reference, add a small captioned viewpoint diagram instead of a second photograph.
- **Reference fallback:** photograph a recognizable subject in the stated view, with enough margin to see full contour/ground contact and no stylized editing. For people, use adult subjects with consent/licensed production, varied bodies/clothing, and no idealized body standard; alternatively use the learner's own mirror view where the map says so.
- **Teaching drawing:** graphite study demonstrating the exact lesson decision. Its subject may resemble but must not trace a source-book plate. Include a small correction, contrast or sequence only when it explains the concept.
- **Technical diagram:** deterministic SVG where exact convergence, axes, eye level or ellipse geometry must remain clear. Pair it with observed drawing when the diagram alone could be mistaken for a rule that overrides a subject.

## Asset-production safeguards and open visual questions

- Photo/reference assets must be separately rights-cleared; model releases and usage scope are reviewed before any human-reference production.
- Distinguish a course's teaching drawing from the subject reference image. Label each with alt text that says what drawing decision becomes easier to understand.
- Prefer deterministic diagrams for eye level, ellipse geometry and perspective convergence; keep the drawn application beside them if the learner might treat a model as universal.
- At phone width, essential labels remain readable and the graphite marks carrying the concept stay distinct. Keep decorative text out of the image.
- No image in this plan should be generated from a source-page image. Source books may inform the principle; prompts specify original subject arrangements and examples.
- Source visual candidates are optional research context. No lesson depends on the learner seeing a copyrighted book illustration.
